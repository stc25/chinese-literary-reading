## Summary

How people are named and referred to, and the main offices and ranks of the Qing and Republican states.

## Personal names

A man of letters had several names, each used in different settings.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 姓 | surname | 嚴 |
| 名 | given name, used by oneself and by superiors | 復 |
| 初名, 原名 | original given name | 宗光 |
| 字 | courtesy name, taken at adulthood, used by friends | 又陵, 幾道 |
| 號 | style, sobriquet | 林紓, 號畏廬 |
| 室名 | studio name | 飲冰室 (Liang Qichao), 秋水軒 (Xu Simei) |
| 筆名 | pen name (Republican period) | 魯迅 |
| 諡 | posthumous name, granted by the court | 曾國藩, 諡文正 |

Women are often recorded only by surname: 王氏 "née Wang". See
[Conventions of biography](../grammar/biography-conventions.md).

**Avoiding names.** It was rude to use a superior's or an elder's given name. A son writes his father's
name with a stroke missing or avoids it; everyone avoided the emperor's personal name (see
[Script and dictionaries](t01-script-and-dictionaries.md)).

## Referring to people

Instead of a name, writers often use:

| Way | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| office + surname | 李中堂, 張香帥 | Grand Secretary Li (Li Hongzhang); Governor-General Zhang (Zhang Zhidong, 號香濤) |
| native place | 南海 (Kang Youwei), 合肥 (Li Hongzhang), 項城 (Yuan Shikai), 湘鄉 (Zeng Guofan), 侯官 (Yan Fu) | the man from … |
| surname + 公 / 君 / 氏 | 曾公, 嚴君, 斯賓塞氏 | Lord Zeng, Mr Yan, Spencer |
| posthumous name | 曾文正公, 李文忠公 | Zeng Guofan, Li Hongzhang |
| honorific | 中堂, 大人, 鈞座 | Your Excellency (see [Honorific and humble forms](../grammar/honorific-humble.md)) |
| given name alone | 復, 紓 | in a biography, after the first mention |

## Qing government

**Central government.**

| Office | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 軍機處 | Grand Council | the inner council of the emperor, from 1730s to 1911 |
| 內閣 | Grand Secretariat | formal channel for documents; its members, the 大學士, were addressed as 中堂 |
| 六部 | the Six Boards | 吏 (Civil Appointments), 戶 (Revenue), 禮 (Rites), 兵 (War), 刑 (Punishments), 工 (Works) |
| 尚書, 侍郎 | president, vice-president | of a board |
| 總理各國事務衙門 (總理衙門) | Office for Foreign Affairs | 1861–1901, replaced by the 外務部 |
| 都察院 | Censorate | officials who could criticise and impeach |
| 翰林院 | Hanlin Academy | the top examination graduates; its members were called 太史 |

From 1901 new ministries (部) were created: 外務部 (1901), 商部 (1903), 學部 (1905), 巡警部 / 民政部 (1905–06),
and in 1906 the boards were reorganised as ministries. In 1911 a cabinet (內閣, with a 總理大臣) replaced
the Grand Council.

**Provincial and local government.**

| Office | English | Responsible for |
|---|---|---|
| 總督 | governor-general | one to three provinces (e.g. 直隸總督, 兩江總督) |
| 巡撫 | governor | one province |
| 布政使 | provincial treasurer | finance and personnel |
| 按察使 | provincial judge | justice |
| 道 (道台) | circuit intendant | a circuit of several prefectures, or a function (customs, grain) |
| 知府 | prefect | a prefecture (府) |
| 知州 | department magistrate | a department (州) |
| 知縣 | county magistrate | a county (縣) |

Special posts include the 北洋大臣 and 南洋大臣 (Commissioners for the Northern and Southern Ports), held by
the governors-general of Zhili and Liangjiang.

**Ranks.** Officials held one of nine ranks (品), each divided into 正 "full" and 從 "secondary": 正一品 is the
highest, 從九品 the lowest. Honorary titles (宮太傅, 太子少保…) and noble ranks (公, 侯, 伯, 子, 男) were
granted on top of office.

## Examinations and degrees

| Degree | Examination | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 童生 | | candidate | not yet passed |
| 生員 (秀才) | 院試, county and prefecture | licentiate | entitled to sit the provincial examination |
| 舉人 | 鄉試, provincial, every three years | provincial graduate | could be appointed to office |
| 進士 | 會試 and 殿試, in the capital | metropolitan graduate | the highest degree; the first is the 狀元 |
| 翰林 | | Hanlin academician | the top 進士 |

Degrees could also be bought (捐納, 捐官), as in Zeng Guofan's letter. The examinations were abolished in 1905
(see [Ending the examinations](../units/u13-qing-memorials-and-edicts/u13-p02-ending-the-examinations.md)).

## Identifying people

- **China Biographical Database (CBDB)**, Harvard and Academia Sinica: names, offices, kinship.
- 中央研究院〈人名權威資料查詢〉 (Academia Sinica Authority Database): names, courtesy names, styles.
- 楊廷福、楊同甫《清人室名別稱字號索引》: courtesy names, styles and studio names of Qing people.
- 《清代官員履歷檔案全編》 and the Qing official registers (縉紳錄) for officials' careers.
- 徐友春《民國人物大辭典》 for Republican figures.
- Howard Boorman, *Biographical Dictionary of Republican China* (1967–79), and Arthur Hummel,
  *Eminent Chinese of the Ch'ing Period* (1943–44), in English.

## Practice

### 1. choice

A writer of 1900 refers to 合肥. Whom does he probably mean?

- [ ] Kang Youwei
- [x] Li Hongzhang
- [ ] Zeng Guofan

### 2. short-answer

Put these degrees in order from lowest to highest: 進士, 生員, 舉人.

**Answer:** 生員, 舉人, 進士.
