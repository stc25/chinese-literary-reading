## Summary

Currencies, weights, lengths, areas and volumes found in period texts, with approximate values.

## Silver and copper

The Qing used two kinds of money: uncoined **silver**, weighed in taels, and **copper** coins.

| Unit | Pinyin | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 兩 | liǎng | tael, about 37 g of silver | the unit of account for taxes, prices and indemnities |
| 錢 | qián | 1/10 tael | also "money" in general |
| 分 | fēn | 1/100 tael | |
| 釐 | lí | 1/1000 tael | 釐金 likin, a transit tax |
| 文 | wén | one copper cash | a round coin with a square hole |
| 串, 弔 | chuàn, diào | a string of cash, nominally 1,000 | often fewer in practice |

There was no single tael. The **Kuping tael** (庫平兩, the Treasury tael, about 37.3 g) was used for
government accounts and indemnities; the **Haikwan tael** (海關兩, the Customs tael, about 37.8 g) for
customs duties; local market taels varied. The exchange rate between silver and copper also varied: in the
mid-nineteenth century a tael bought between about 1,000 and 2,000 cash.

The Treaty of Shimonoseki's indemnity, 庫平銀二萬萬兩 "200 million Kuping taels", was about 7,450 tonnes of
silver.

## Dollars

Foreign silver dollars, especially the **Mexican dollar** (鷹洋, 洋銀, 洋錢), circulated on the coast from the
eighteenth century and were reckoned at about 0.72 tael (七錢二分). The Qing and the Republic then minted
their own dollars: the 龍洋 "dragon dollar" and, from 1914, the 袁大頭 "Yuan Shikai dollar".

| Unit | Pinyin | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 元, 圓 | yuán | dollar |
| 角 | jiǎo | 1/10 dollar (ten cents) |
| 分 | fēn | 1/100 dollar (a cent) |

In 1933 the Republic abolished the tael (廢兩改元), and in 1935 replaced silver with a paper currency (法幣).

The Treaty of Nanjing's indemnity was reckoned in dollars: 洋銀三百萬圓 (see
[Opium and silver](../units/u06-prepositions/u06-p03-opium-and-silver.md)).

## Weights

| Unit | Pinyin | Value | English trade name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 斤 | jīn | 16 兩, about 597 g (about 1⅓ lb) | catty |
| 兩 | liǎng | 1/16 斤, about 37 g | tael |
| 擔 | dàn | 100 斤, about 60 kg (133⅓ lb) | picul |

From 1929 the 市制 "market system" standardised the 市斤 at 500 g, divided into 10 市兩.

## Length and area

| Unit | Pinyin | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 寸 | cùn | 1/10 尺, about 3.2 cm |
| 尺 | chǐ | about 32 cm (the Qing 營造尺) |
| 丈 | zhàng | 10 尺, about 3.2 m |
| 里 | lǐ | 1,800 尺, about 576 m (roughly half a kilometre) |
| 畝 | mǔ | about 614 m² (about 1/6 acre) |
| 頃 | qǐng | 100 畝, about 6.1 hectares |

Local values of the 尺, 里 and 畝 varied widely. From 1929 the 市尺 was 1/3 m, the 市里 500 m and the 市畝
666⅔ m².

Foreign units were given Chinese names: 英里 "English mile", 碼 "yard", 洋里 "foreign li" (used for kilometres
or miles, depending on the country; see [Paris to London](../units/u16-the-press-and-new-vocabulary/u16-p03-paris-to-london.md)).

## Volume

| Unit | Pinyin | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 升 | shēng | about 1 litre |
| 斗 | dǒu | 10 升, about 10 litres |
| 石 | shí, later dàn | 10 斗, about 100 litres (of grain) |

The 石 was the unit of grain tax and of official salaries. As a weight of grain it was reckoned at roughly
120–160 斤 depending on the grain.

## Practice

A Mexican dollar was worth about 七錢二分. Express this as a fraction of a tael.

**Answer:** 7 錢 + 2 分 = 0.72 tael.
