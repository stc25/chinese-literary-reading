## Summary

Plain-English definitions of the grammatical terms used in this course.

## Gloss labels

Glosses may begin with one of these labels:

| Label | Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| *n.* | noun | a word for a person, thing, place or idea | 國 "state", 民 "people" |
| *v.* | verb | a word for an action, process or state | 行 "to go", 有 "to have" |
| *adj.* | adjective | a word that describes; in Chinese it can act as a verb ("to be big") | 大, 新 |
| *adv.* | adverb | a word that modifies a verb or adjective | 皆 "all", 甚 "very", 不 "not" |
| *pron.* | pronoun | a word that stands for a noun | 吾 "I", 其 "his, its", 之 "it" |
| *part.* | particle | a function word with no meaning of its own, which marks grammar or mood | 也, 矣, 乎, 之 (joining) |
| *prep.* | preposition | a word that introduces a noun phrase of place, time, means or cause | 於 "at", 以 "with", 自 "from" |
| *conj.* | conjunction | a word that joins words or clauses | 而 "and, but", 則 "then", 雖 "although" |
| *interj.* | interjection | an exclamation | 嗚呼 "alas" |
| *num.* | numeral | a number | 三, 百 |
| *mw.* | measure word | a word used between a number and a noun | 隻 (for boats), 箇 |

## Terms

**Allusion (典故).** A reference to an earlier text or story, which the reader is expected to recognise.
See [Allusion](../grammar/allusion.md).

**Aspect.** Whether an action is completed, ongoing or about to happen, marked in literary Chinese by 已, 既,
將, 方, 矣. See [Aspect markers](../grammar/aspect-markers.md).

**Causative.** A verb used to mean "to make (someone) do or be": 活之 "to make him live, to save him".

**Clause.** A group of words with its own predicate, part of a sentence.

**Comment.** What is said about the topic. See *topic*.

**Copula.** A word that links subject and predicate, "to be": 為, 乃, 即, 係; the vernacular 是. Literary Chinese
often does without: ……者，……也. See [The copula 為](../grammar/wei-copula.md).

**Final particle.** A particle at the end of a sentence that marks its mood: 也 (statement), 矣 (change, conclusion),
乎 (question), 哉 (exclamation). See [也 at the end of a sentence](../grammar/ye-final.md).

**Head.** The main word of a phrase, which the other words modify: in 富強之國 "a rich and strong state", 國 is the
head.

**Modal verb.** A verb that expresses possibility, necessity or permission: 能, 可, 得, 須, 宜, 應. See
[Modal verbs](../grammar/modal-verbs.md).

**Modifier.** A word or phrase that describes or limits another (the head). In Chinese the modifier comes first,
often joined by 之 (vernacular 的).

**Negation.** Words meaning "not": 不, 弗, 未 "not yet", 非 "is not", 無 "there is no", 勿 / 毋 "do not". See
[Negation with 不](../grammar/bu-negation.md).

**Nominaliser.** A word that turns a verb or a clause into a noun phrase: 者 "the one who", 所 "that which", 之
between subject and verb. See [者 as a nominaliser](../grammar/zhe-nominaliser.md).

**Object.** The person or thing that a verb acts on: 讀書 "read books". A pronoun object may come before the verb
in negative sentences: 莫之擇 "no one selects it".

**Parallelism (對偶, 排比).** Matching phrases or sentences of the same length and structure. See
[Parallelism](../grammar/parallelism.md).

**Passive.** A construction in which the subject undergoes the action: 為……所……, 見, 被. See
[Passives](../grammar/passives.md).

**Predicate.** What is said about the subject: the verb or adjective with its objects and modifiers.

**Putative use (意動).** A verb or adjective used to mean "to consider (something) as": 奇之 "to think it
remarkable".

**Register.** The level of style: classical, literary, plain literary or vernacular. See
[Mixed registers](../grammar/mixed-register.md).

**Rhetorical question.** A question asked not for an answer but to make a point: 豈……乎 "surely not …?" See
[Rhetorical questions](../grammar/rhetorical-questions.md).

**Split construction.** A pattern in two parts with other words between them: 非……不……, 以……為……. In glosses it is
written with … between the parts.

**Subject.** The person or thing about which the predicate says something. In literary Chinese the subject is
often left out when it is clear from context.

**Topic.** What a sentence is about, placed first and often marked by 者 or 也; the rest of the sentence is the
comment. See [Topic and comment](../grammar/topic-comment.md).

**Word class.** The category of a word (noun, verb, …). In literary Chinese many words can move between classes:
a noun can be used as a verb (君天子 "to treat the Son of Heaven as lord"). See
[Word classes](../grammar/word-classes.md).

**Vernacular (白話).** Written Chinese based on modern speech. See
[Vernacular elements in literary prose](../grammar/vernacular-elements.md).
