## Introduction

The Treaty of Tianjin (1858), signed during the Second Opium War, extended the treaty system
begun at Nanjing: foreign envoys in Beijing, more open ports, travel in the interior,
missionaries. Two short articles near the end concern **language**. One makes the English
text of all treaties the authority. The other forbids Chinese officials to call the British
夷 "barbarians".

Compare them with the separate article of the Treaty of Shimonoseki in
[Korea and Taiwan](u15-p02-korea-and-taiwan.md): there a neutral third language settled
disputes; here one side's language prevails.

## Text

第一款：前壬寅年七月二十四日江寧所定和約仍留照行。廣東所定善後舊約並通商章程現在更章，既經併入新約，所有舊約作為廢紙。

第五十款：嗣後英國文書俱用英字書寫，暫時仍以漢文配送，俟中國選派學生學習英文、英語熟習，即不用配送漢文。自今以後，遇有文詞辯論之處，總以英文作為正義。此次定約，漢、英文書詳細較對無訛，亦照此例。

第五十一款：嗣後各式公文，無論京外，內敘大英國官民，自不得提書夷字。

## Translation

Article 1: The peace treaty concluded earlier at Jiangning on the 24th day of the 7th month of the renyin year remains in force. The supplementary treaty concluded in Guangdong and the trade regulations are now revised; since they have been incorporated into the new treaty, all the old treaties are null and void.

Article 50: From now on, British communications shall all be written in English. For the time being they will still be accompanied by Chinese; as soon as China has selected and sent students to learn English and they are proficient in the language, Chinese will no longer be sent with them. From now on, whenever there is a dispute about wording, the English shall in all cases be taken as the correct meaning. The treaty now concluded, of which the Chinese and English texts have been carefully checked and found without error, is also subject to this rule.

Article 51: From now on, in official documents of all kinds, whether in the capital or the provinces, the character 夷 may not, of course, be written in referring to British officials and subjects.

## Notes

**Comparing the English.** The English text of article 50 (Article L) says, in substance,
that British official communications to the Chinese authorities "shall henceforth be written in
English", accompanied "for the present" by a Chinese version, and that "in the event of there
being any difference of meaning between the English and Chinese text, the English Government
will hold the sense as expressed in the English text to be the correct sense". The provision
applies to the treaty itself.

| | Chinese | English |
|---|---|---|
| until when Chinese is sent | 俟中國選派學生學習英文、英語熟習 "until Chinese students have learnt English" | "for the present" |
| who decides | 總以英文作為正義 "English is in all cases the correct meaning" | "the English Government will hold the sense … to be the correct sense" |

The two texts do not say quite the same thing. The Chinese adds a condition for ending
the translations (Chinese students learning English), which the English does not mention. The English
makes the British government the judge, which the Chinese does not mention. Under article 50 itself, the
English version would prevail.

**夷.** Article 51 bans the character 夷 yí for the British. It had been the ordinary word for
foreigners in Qing documents (as in 以夷制夷 "use barbarians to control barbarians"). The
British read it as "barbarian" and an insult; Chinese officials argued it simply meant
"foreigner". After 1858 official documents used 洋 yáng "overseas" or 外國 "foreign
country" instead: hence 洋務 "foreign affairs", 洋人 "foreigners". The English text of
Article LI glosses the character as "barbarian". A single character in a treaty shows how
language itself became a matter of sovereignty and equality.

**Old and new treaties.** Article 1 keeps the Treaty of Nanjing (前壬寅年…江寧所定和約) and
cancels the 1843 supplementary treaty, which had been absorbed into the new one. 仍留照行
"remains in force" and 作為廢紙 "null and void, waste paper" are the treaty words for keeping
and cancelling.

**Dating by the cycle.** The Treaty of Nanjing is cited by the cyclical year 壬寅 (1842) and
the lunar date, not by reign year. See [Dates and calendars](../../toolkit/t02-dates-and-calendars.md).

**自不得.** 自 "of course, naturally" softens the prohibition: the ban is presented as
self-evident, not imposed.

## Exercises

### 1. choice

According to article 50, which text decides a dispute about wording?

- [ ] the Chinese text
- [x] the English text
- [ ] whichever text was signed first

### 2. short-answer

What condition does the Chinese text of article 50 give for no longer sending Chinese versions? Is it in the English?

**Answer:** When China has trained students who are proficient in English. The English text only says "for the present" and does not mention it.

### 3. short-answer

What words replaced 夷 for foreigners and foreign things after 1858?

**Answer:** 洋 (洋人, 洋務) and 外國.

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate article 51.

**Text:** 嗣後各式公文無論京外內敘大英國官民自不得提書夷字

**Answer:** 嗣後各式公文，無論京外，內敘大英國官民，自不得提書夷字。

### 5. translate

Translate 自今以後，遇有文詞辯論之處，總以英文作為正義。

**Answer:** From now on, whenever there is a dispute about wording, the English shall in all cases be taken as the correct meaning.
