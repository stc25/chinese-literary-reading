## Introduction

Zeng Guofan, the general who defeated the Taiping and the model Confucian statesman of the
late Qing, wrote more than a thousand letters to his family. Printed after his death as
《曾文正公家書》, they were read for generations as a guide to conduct, and they are a good
introduction to family letters.

This passage gives parts of two letters, twenty years apart. In the first, written in 1842 when
he was a young official in Beijing, Zeng writes **up** to his parents. In the second, written in
1862 when he was commanding the war against the Taiping, he writes **down** to his sons. Compare
how each letter opens and closes, how Zeng names himself and the others, and the tone.

## Text

男國藩跪稟父母親大人萬福金安：

六月二十八日接到家書，係三月二十四日所發。知十九日四弟得生子，男等合室相慶。四妹生產雖難，然血暈亦是常事。且此次既能保全，則下次較為容易。男未得信時，常以為慮，既得此信，如釋重負。……

逆夷在江蘇濨擾，於六月十一日攻陷鎮江，有大船數十只在大江游戈。江寧、楊州二府頗可危慮。然而天不降災，聖人在上，故京師人心鎮定。……

男每接家信，嫌其不詳，嗣後更願詳示。

男謹稟。七月初四日。

字諭紀澤、紀鴻兒：

……爾近來寫字，總失之薄弱，骨力不堅勁，墨氣不豐腴，與爾身體向來輕字之弊，正是一路毛病。爾當用油紙摹顏字之〈郭家廟〉、柳字之〈琅琊碑〉、〈元祕塔〉，以藥其病。日日留心，專從「厚重」二字上用功。否則字質太薄，即體質亦因之更輕矣。人之氣質，由於天生，本難改變，惟讀書則可變化氣質。古之精相法者，并言讀書可以變換骨相。欲求變之之法，總須先立堅卓之志。即以余生平言之，三十歲前最好吃煙，片刻不離，至道光壬寅十一月二十一日立志戒煙，至今不再吃。四十六歲以前作事無恆，近五年深以為戒，現在大小事均尚有恆。即此二端，可見無事不可變也。爾於「厚重」二字，須立志變改。古稱金丹換骨，余謂立志即丹也。……

滌生示。四月二十四日。

## Translation

Your son Guofan, kneeling, reports to his honoured father and mother, wishing you every blessing and good health:

On the twenty-eighth of the sixth month I received the letter from home, which was sent on the twenty-fourth of the third month. I learnt that on the nineteenth my fourth younger brother had a son born, and the whole household here rejoiced. Fourth sister's childbirth was difficult, but fainting from loss of blood is a common thing. And since this time she came through safely, next time it will be easier. Before I had the letter I was always anxious; now that I have it, it is as if a heavy load had been lifted. ……

The rebellious barbarians are harassing Jiangsu. On the eleventh of the sixth month they captured Zhenjiang, and several dozen large ships are cruising on the Yangzi. The prefectures of Jiangning and Yangzhou are in considerable danger. But Heaven sends no calamity, and with a sage on the throne, people in the capital are calm. ……

Each time I receive a letter from home, I find it not detailed enough. From now on, please tell me everything in detail.

Your son respectfully reports. Fourth day of the seventh month.

Written instructions to my sons Jize and Jihong:

…… Your handwriting lately always errs on the side of thinness and weakness: its structure is not strong, its ink not rich. It is the same kind of fault as the "lightness" your body has always had. You should trace on oiled paper Yan Zhenqing's *Guo Family Temple* and Liu Gongquan's *Langya Stele* and *Xuanmi Pagoda* to cure the fault. Pay attention every day, and work at the two words "solid and weighty". Otherwise, if the substance of your writing is too thin, even your constitution will grow lighter because of it. A person's temperament is innate and hard to change; only reading can transform it. Those skilled in physiognomy in ancient times even said that reading could change one's bone structure. If you seek a way to change, you must first make a firm and outstanding resolve. To take my own life as an example: before the age of thirty I was very fond of smoking and never without it for a moment; on the twenty-first of the eleventh month of the renyin year of Daoguang I resolved to give it up, and I have not smoked since. Before forty-six I lacked perseverance in what I did; for the last five years I have taken this as a serious warning, and now I am fairly persevering in matters great and small. From these two matters you can see that there is nothing that cannot be changed. As for "solid and weighty", you must resolve to change. The ancients said the golden elixir transforms the bones; I say resolve is the elixir. ……

Instructions from Disheng. Twenty-fourth day of the fourth month.

## Notes

**Up and down.**

| | To his parents (1842) | To his sons (1862) |
|---|---|---|
| opening | 男國藩跪稟父母親大人萬福金安 | 字諭紀澤、紀鴻兒 |
| writer called | 男 "your son", given name 國藩 | 余 "I", style name 滌生 |
| addressee called | 父母親大人 | 爾 "you", 兒 |
| verb of writing | 稟 "report (upwards)", 跪 "kneeling" | 諭 "instruct", 示 "show" (downwards) |
| closing | 男謹稟 | 滌生示 |
| tone | dutiful news; asks for more detail | advice, examples from his own life, commands (爾當, 須) |

The forty-year-old Zeng, then one of the most powerful men in China, writes 爾 to his sons; the
thirty-year-old Zeng writes 男 to his parents and kneels on paper. See
[Honorific and humble forms](../../grammar/honorific-humble.md).

**Signing with a style name.** To his parents Zeng uses his given name (國藩): a son is named by his
given name to his elders. To his sons he signs with his style 滌生, the name he used among friends
and juniors. See [Conventions of biography](../../grammar/biography-conventions.md) on 名, 字 and 號.

**Dates in letters.** Family letters give only month and day (七月初四日). The year must be worked
out from the contents: 逆夷……攻陷鎮江 dates the first letter to 1842, the Opium War. Editors
add the year, as the Wikisource compilation does in brackets.

**逆夷.** In 1842 an official could call the British 逆夷 "rebellious barbarians". After the Treaty of
Tianjin (1858) the word 夷 was banned in official documents; see
[The English text prevails](../u15-law-and-treaties/u15-p03-the-english-text-prevails.md).

**A small link.** The day in 1842 when Zeng gave up smoking (道光壬寅十一月二十一日) fell four months
after the first letter. The two letters belong to the same life.

**Calligraphy and character.** For Zeng handwriting showed character: thin, weak writing went with a
"light" person, and both could be changed by effort. 變化氣質 "transforming one's temperament" is a
Neo-Confucian phrase (Zhang Zai, Zhu Xi). See [Philosophical vocabulary](../../grammar/philosophical-terms.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

How does Zeng refer to himself in the letter to his parents?

- [x] 男 and his given name 國藩
- [ ] 余 and his style name 滌生
- [ ] 弟

### 2. short-answer

Which verbs show the direction of each letter?

**Answer:** 稟 "report" (with 跪 "kneeling") in the letter to his parents, upwards; 諭 "instruct" and 示 "show" in the letter to his sons, downwards.

### 3. short-answer

What two examples from his own life does Zeng give to show that one can change?

**Answer:** He gave up smoking in 1842 and has not smoked since; and after forty-six he overcame his lack of perseverance.

### 4. translate

Translate 人之氣質，由於天生，本難改變，惟讀書則可變化氣質。

**Answer:** A person's temperament is innate and hard to change; only reading can transform it.
