## Introduction

In the summer of 1894 a 28-year-old doctor from Guangdong travelled to Tianjin with a long
letter for Li Hongzhang, the most powerful official in China. The doctor was Sun Wen, later
known as Sun Yat-sen. His letter proposed reforms: "that people use their talents to the
full, the land its resources, things their uses, and goods flow freely". Li did not receive
him. A few months later Sun founded the 興中會 in Honolulu, his first revolutionary society.

Whatever its content, the letter had to follow the form of a petition from a commoner to a
great official. Read its opening and its closing. Mark every word that raises Li (中堂, 鈞座,
垂鑒) and every word that lowers Sun (竊, 管見, 芻蕘, 愚夫, 冒昧).

## Text

宮太傅爵中堂鈞座：

敬稟者，竊文籍隸粵東，世居香邑。曾于香港考授英國醫士。幼嘗遊學外洋，於泰西之語言、文字、政治、禮俗，與夫天算、輿地之學，格物化學之理，皆略有所窺；而尤留心於其富國強兵之道，化民成俗之規。至於時局變遷之故，睦鄰交際之宜，輒能洞其竅奧。當今民氣日開，四方畢集，正值國家勵精圖治之時，朝廷勤求政理之日，每欲以管見所知，指陳時事，上諸當道，以備芻蕘之採。嗣以人微言輕，末敢遽達。……

伏維我中堂佐治以來，無利不興，無弊不革，艱鉅險阻，在所不辭。……用敢不辭冒昧，侃侃而談，為生民請命。伏祈採擇施行，天下幸甚，肅此具稟，恭叩鈞綏，伏維垂鑒！

文謹稟。

## Translation

To the seat of His Excellency the Grand Secretary, Grand Tutor of the Heir Apparent, Earl:

I respectfully report that I, Wen, venture to say that I am registered as a native of eastern Guangdong, and my family has lived for generations in Xiangshan. I qualified in Hong Kong as a doctor of the English kind. In my youth I studied overseas, and of the West's languages, writing, government and customs, its astronomy, mathematics and geography, and its principles of physics and chemistry, I have had some glimpse; and I paid particular attention to its ways of enriching the state and strengthening the army, and its rules for educating the people and forming customs. As for the reasons for the changes in the times and the proper conduct of relations with neighbouring states, I have always been able to see into their secrets. Now, when the people's spirit grows more enlightened every day and talent gathers from all quarters, when the state is striving hard for good government and the court seeking earnestly the principles of government, I have often wished to set out my narrow view of current affairs and submit it to those in power, to be available for selection like the words of grass-cutters. But because my person is humble and my words carry little weight, I did not dare to send it hastily. ……

I consider that since Your Excellency began to assist in governing, there has been no benefit you have not promoted and no abuse you have not reformed, and you have never declined difficulty or danger. …… Therefore I dare, despite my presumption, to speak frankly and plead on behalf of the people. I pray that you will select these proposals and put them into effect; the whole country would be most fortunate. I respectfully submit this petition, respectfully wish Your Excellency well, and pray that you will deign to read it!

Respectfully submitted by Wen.

## Notes

**The frame of a petition.** A letter to a superior has a fixed frame. The content sits inside it.

| Part | Here | Function |
|---|---|---|
| address (稱謂) | 宮太傅爵中堂 | titles of the recipient |
| address term (提稱語) | 鈞座 | "to your seat": the letter is placed before him, not handed to him |
| opening (啟事語) | 敬稟者 | "I respectfully report": 稟 is used only upwards |
| self-introduction | 竊文籍隸粵東 | humble 竊, given name only |
| praise of the recipient | 伏維我中堂佐治以來…… | |
| request | 伏祈採擇施行 | |
| closing (結束語) | 肅此具稟 | "I respectfully submit this" |
| good wishes (祝頌語) | 恭叩鈞綏 | "respectfully wish you well": 鈞 for a high official |
| final request | 伏維垂鑒 | "deign to read" |
| signature | 文謹稟 | given name + "respectfully submits" |

See [Letter formulas](../../grammar/epistolary-formulas.md).

**Raising and lowering.** Sun calls himself only 文, his given name, and his views 管見 "a view
through a tube" and 芻蕘之採 "what grass-cutters say" (from the *Book of Songs*: 詢于芻蕘 "consult
even the grass-cutters"). He 竊 "ventures", 伏 "prostrates himself", and 不辭冒昧 "risks
presumption". Li is 中堂, his seat is 鈞座, his wellbeing 鈞綏, his reading 垂鑒 "looking down".
None of this is personal: it is the grammar of writing upwards. See
[Honorific and humble forms](../../grammar/honorific-humble.md).

**The honorific gap.** In the transcription a blank space stands before 鈞綏. Like the elevation
of the emperor's words in [Qing memorials](../u13-qing-memorials-and-edicts/unit.md), this gap
(挪抬 nuótái) honours the recipient: the word that refers to him is set apart. In the course
text the gap is closed; when you read the original, look for it.

**Modesty and claims.** Within the humble frame, Sun claims a great deal: knowledge of Western
languages, government, science, and 輒能洞其竅奧 "I have always been able to see into their
secrets". The formulas make the boldness acceptable.

**稟.** 稟 is "to report to a superior": a son to his father (稟父母), a subordinate to an official,
a commoner to the state. A letter so written is a 稟 or 稟帖. Compare 呈 (Republican petitions,
[U14](../u14-republican-documents/unit.md)) and 奏 (memorials to the throne).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What does 敬稟者 tell you about the relationship between writer and recipient?

- [ ] They are equals.
- [x] The writer is writing upwards, to a superior.
- [ ] The writer is giving an order.

### 2. short-answer

Find three expressions in which Sun lowers himself or his views.

**Answer:** Any three of: 竊, 管見, 芻蕘之採, 人微言輕, 不辭冒昧, his use of his given name 文, 伏維, 伏祈, 謹稟.

### 3. short-answer

What do 鈞座, 鈞綏 and 垂鑒 have in common?

**Answer:** They refer to Li respectfully: his seat, his wellbeing and his reading. 鈞 is used for high officials, and 垂 "to let down" implies that he looks down from above.

### 4. translate

Translate 用敢不辭冒昧，侃侃而談，為生民請命。

**Answer:** Therefore I dare, despite my presumption, to speak frankly and plead on behalf of the people.
