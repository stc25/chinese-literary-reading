## Introduction

Formal letters are full of polite formulas that carry social meaning: who is writing to whom, and how far up or down. In this unit you read three kinds of letter: Sun Yat-sen's petition to Li Hongzhang (1894), written upwards from a commoner to the greatest official of the day; two family letters by Zeng Guofan, one to his parents (1842) and one to his sons (1862); and two letters between friends from 《秋水軒尺牘》, the most popular letter-writing manual of the nineteenth century. For a diary, see Xue Fucheng's entries in [Paris to London](../u16-the-press-and-new-vocabulary/u16-p03-paris-to-london.md).

## Objectives

By the end of this unit you will be able to:

- identify the parts of a formal letter
- read honorific and humble forms
- tell from its formulas whether a letter is written upwards, downwards or between equals
