## Introduction

The 澄衷蒙學堂字課圖說 ("Illustrated Character Lessons of the Chengzhong Primary School", 1901)
was one of the first school textbooks of its kind in China. It explains about 3,300 characters
one by one, in short sentences of simple literary Chinese written for children.

These sentences come from the entry for 星 "star". The first paragraph is the short
definition; each of the others is the first sentence of a sub-entry on one planet.

## Text

天空諸曜曰星。

水星即辰星也。

金星即太白星也。夜見西方曰長庚，朝見東方曰啟明。

地球亦行星也。

火星即熒惑也。

木星即歲星也。

土星即填星也。

## Translation

The heavenly bodies of the sky are called 星 "stars".

Mercury is Chenxing.

Venus is Taibai. When it appears in the west at night it is called Changgeng; when it appears in the east in the morning it is called Qiming.

The earth, too, is a planet.

Mars is Yinghuo.

Jupiter is Suixing.

Saturn is Zhenxing.

## Literal translation

Sky all-the luminaries are-called star.

Water-star is Chen-star 也.

Metal-star is Taibai-star 也. Night appear west, called Changgeng; morning appear east, called Qiming.

Earth also planet 也.

Fire-star is Yinghuo 也.

Wood-star is Year-star 也.

Earth-star is Zhen-star 也.

## Notes

**Sentences without "is".** 水星即辰星也 means "Mercury is Chenxing", but there is no
word for "is" that works like the English verb. The pattern is **A (即) B 也**: put two
nouns side by side and close the sentence with 也. 即 "precisely, that is" is optional and
adds emphasis; 地球亦行星也 uses 亦 "also" instead. See
[也 ending a nominal sentence](../../grammar/ye-final.md).

**Words without endings.** Nothing in a word shows whether it is a noun, a verb or a
time expression. In 夜見西方, 夜 "night" and 朝 "morning" stand before the verb 見, so
they tell you *when*: "at night", "in the morning". The position decides. See
[Word classes and flexibility](../../grammar/word-classes.md).

**One character, two readings.** 見 usually means "to see" (jiàn), but here it means
"to appear" and is read xiàn. Glosses always give the reading that fits the context.

**Two sets of names.** The primer explains the names 水星, 金星, 火星, 木星, 土星
(from the Five Phases: water, metal, fire, wood, earth) by giving older names
for the same planets. Writing that the earth "too is a planet" (地球亦行星也) was itself a lesson in the new astronomy.

## Exercises

### 1. choice

In 水星即辰星**也**, what does 也 do?

- [ ] It means "also"
- [x] It closes a statement of the type "A is B"
- [ ] It asks a question

**Explanation:** Literary Chinese often has no verb "to be". Two nouns side by side, closed by 也, make the statement "A is B".

### 2. choice

In **夜**見西方曰長庚, what is 夜 doing?

- [ ] It is the subject: "the night appears in the west"
- [x] It tells you when: "at night"
- [ ] It is the object: "it sees the night"

**Explanation:** 夜 stands before the verb 見, so it says when the action happens. Position, not form, tells you what a word is doing.

### 3. short-answer

What are Venus's names as the evening star and as the morning star?

**Answer:** In the evening, in the west, it is 長庚 Changgeng; in the morning, in the east, it is 啟明 Qiming.

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 金星即太白星也夜見西方曰長庚朝見東方曰啟明

**Answer:** 金星即太白星也。夜見西方曰長庚，朝見東方曰啟明。

**Explanation:** 也 ends the first statement. The next two clauses are parallel (夜見西方…，朝見東方…), so a comma separates them.

### 5. translate

Translate 地球亦行星也。

**Answer:** The earth, too, is a planet.
