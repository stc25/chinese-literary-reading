## Introduction

This passage is harder than the others in this unit. It is fully glossed, and it tells
you something about the grammar you are learning.

In the guide to the use of his primer (凡例), Liu Shuping explains that each character is
labelled with its word class. The nine classes are those of 馬建忠 Ma Jianzhong's
《馬氏文通》 (1898), the first grammar of Chinese modelled on European grammars. Liu
argues that the new classes are not really foreign.

## Text

類字：如名、代、動、靜、狀、介、連、助、嘆九類。西人以此為聯句屬文之要規，實即中國虛實呆活四義所擴充也。

## Original text

類字。如名代動靜狀介連助嘆九類。西人以此為聯句屬文之要規。實即中國虛實呆活四義所擴充也。

## Translation

Classifying the characters: for example, the nine classes of nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, particles and interjections. Westerners regard this as the essential rule for joining phrases and composing writing, but in fact it is simply an expansion of China's four categories of "empty" and "full", "dead" and "live" words.

## Literal translation

Classify characters: such-as noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, particle, interjection nine classes. Westerners take this as join-phrase compose-writing 之 essential-rule, in-fact is China empty-full dead-live four-categories that-which-expanded 也.

## Notes

**The terms.** Each class name is one character, short for a two-character term: 名 for
名字, 動 for 動字, and so on. 字 here means "word". This course uses the English terms,
which are defined in [Grammatical terms](../../toolkit/t07-grammatical-terms.md).

**Older Chinese categories.** Chinese scholars had long divided words into 虛字 "empty
words" (particles and other function words) and 實字 "full words" (words with content),
and into 死字 / 呆字 "dead words" and 活字 "live words" (roughly, things and actions). Liu claims these four
categories already contained the European classes.

**A whole sentence as "A is B".** The last sentence ends in 也 like the planet definitions in
[The planets](u01-p01-the-planets.md): its subject (此 "this", carried over from the first
part) "is in fact (即) what has been expanded from China's four categories". See
[也 ending a nominal sentence](../../grammar/ye-final.md).

**Grammar from later units.** 以…為 "to take … as", 之 and 所 are glossed here and taught later:
see [以](../../grammar/yi-preposition.md), [之 joining modifier and noun](../../grammar/zhi-attributive.md)
and [所 + verb](../../grammar/suo.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

In this passage, what does **名** stand for?

- [ ] a personal name
- [x] a noun
- [ ] fame

**Explanation:** 名 is short for 名字 "naming word", the first of the nine word classes.

### 2. short-answer

Which older Chinese categories does Liu say the nine Western classes are expanded from?

**Answer:** 虛實呆活: "empty" and "full" words, "dead" and "live" words.

### 3. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 西人以此為聯句屬文之要規實即中國虛實呆活四義所擴充也

**Answer:** 西人以此為聯句屬文之要規，實即中國虛實呆活四義所擴充也。

**Explanation:** 實 "in fact" begins the second half, which answers the first.

### 4. translate

Translate 西人以此為聯句屬文之要規。

**Answer:** Westerners regard this as the essential rule for joining phrases and composing writing.
