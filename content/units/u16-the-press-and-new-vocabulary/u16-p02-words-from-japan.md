## Introduction

By 1905 Chinese readers were flooded with new terms, most of them from Japanese translations
of Western books. Some writers used them eagerly; others, attached to the classical language,
rejected them. Wang Guowei, whose 人間詞話 you read in
[Parallelism, allusion and rhythm](../u12-parallelism-and-allusion/unit.md), had studied in
Japan and read German philosophy. In this essay he argues that new learning needs new words,
and that the Japanese terms are generally better than the ones Chinese translators invent,
including Yan Fu's.

The essay is itself an example of what it describes: literary Chinese full of the new
vocabulary, with English words written in Latin letters in the middle of the text.

## Text

如上文所說，言語者，思想之代表也，故新思想之輸入，即新言語輸入之意味也。十年以前，西洋學術之輸入，限於形而下學之方面，故雖有新字新語，於文學上尚未有顯著之影響也。數年以來，形上之學，漸入於中國，而又有一日本焉，爲之中間之驛騎，於是日本所造譯西語之漢文，以混混之勢，而侵入我國之文學界。好奇者濫用之，泥古者唾棄之，二者皆非也。夫普通之文字中，固無事於新奇之語也；至於講一學，治一藝，則非增新語不可。而日本之學者，既先我而定之矣，則沿而用之，何不可之有？故非甚不妥者，吾人固無以創造爲也。

侯官嚴氏，今日以創造學語名者也。嚴氏造語之工者固多，而其不當者亦復不少。茲筆其最著者，如Evolution之爲「天演」也，Sympathy之爲「善相感」也。而天演之於進化，善相感之於同情，其對Evolution與Sympathy之本義，孰得孰失，孰明孰昧，凡稍有外國語之知識者，寧俟終朝而决哉！……竊謂節取日人之譯語，有數便焉：因襲之易，不如創造之難，一也；兩國學術有交通之便，無扞格之虞，二也。

## Translation

As said above, language is the expression of thought; so the import of new thought means the import of new language. Ten years ago, Western learning was imported only in the field of the applied and natural sciences, so although there were new characters and new terms, they had no notable influence on letters. In the last few years theoretical learning has gradually entered China, and Japan has served as the messenger between, so that the Chinese terms coined in Japan to translate Western languages have invaded our world of letters in a torrent. Those who love novelty overuse them; those who cling to antiquity spurn them. Both are wrong. In ordinary writing there is certainly no need for novel terms; but to expound a science or practise an art, new terms must be added. And since the Japanese scholars have already fixed them before us, what is wrong with adopting and using them? So unless they are quite inappropriate, there is no point in our coining our own.

Mr Yan of Houguan is famous today for coining terms. Many of the terms he has coined are apt, but not a few are inappropriate. Let me note the best-known: Evolution rendered as 天演, Sympathy as 善相感. Compare 天演 with 進化 and 善相感 with 同情: as to which is right and which wrong, which clear and which obscure as a rendering of the original meaning of Evolution and Sympathy, anyone with a little knowledge of foreign languages would hardly need a whole morning to decide! … I humbly think that adopting the Japanese translated terms selectively has several advantages. First, taking them over is easy, while coining is hard. Second, the learning of the two countries can communicate easily, without the risk of incompatibility.

## Notes

**The argument.**

1. Language expresses thought, so new thought needs new words.
2. Japan has already translated Western terms into characters.
3. Using them is better than rejecting them (泥古者) or overusing them (好奇者).
4. Even Yan Fu's coinages are worse than the Japanese terms.
5. Using the Japanese terms is easy, and keeps Chinese and Japanese scholarship compatible.

**Two ways to make a new term.**

| Way | Example | Who |
|---|---|---|
| coin from classical Chinese | 天演 "heavenly evolution", 善相感, 群學 "sociology", 計學 "economics" | Yan Fu |
| adopt the Japanese term (often coined in Japan from classical words) | 進化, 同情, 社會學, 經濟學 | Japanese translators |

History proved Wang right: 進化, 同情, 社會, 經濟 are the modern words; 天演, 群學 and 計學
survive only in the history of ideas. When you meet 天演 or 群 in a text of the 1890s, think
of the modern equivalents, but do not assume the meaning is identical.

**Latin letters in Chinese.** Wang writes the English words Evolution and Sympathy in the
middle of his Chinese sentences. This was new in the 1900s and marks a writer who expected his
readers to know a foreign language. Elsewhere in the essay he adds English equivalents in
brackets, such as 綜括（Generalization）.

**形而上 and 形而下.** From the 易經 (繫辭): 形而上者謂之道，形而下者謂之器 "what is above
form is called the Way; what is below form is called the vessel". Wang uses them for theory
(philosophy) and the applied sciences. 形而上學 became the modern term for "metaphysics", again via Japanese.

**Naming by native place.** 侯官嚴氏 "Mr Yan of Houguan" names Yan Fu by his county (in Fujian),
a polite way of referring to a contemporary. Compare 南海康氏 for Kang Youwei.

**Listing advantages.** …，一也；…，二也 is the counting pattern you met in Yan Fu's own
preface in [Faithful, comprehensible, elegant](../u12-parallelism-and-allusion/u12-p01-faithful-fluent-elegant.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What role does Wang give to Japan?

- [ ] It invented Western learning.
- [x] It was the messenger (驛騎) that passed Western terms, translated into characters, to China.
- [ ] It forbade the import of new terms.

### 2. short-answer

Which two groups does Wang criticise, and for what?

**Answer:** 好奇者 "those who love novelty", for overusing new terms; and 泥古者 "those who cling to antiquity", for spurning them.

### 3. short-answer

Give Yan Fu's term and the Japanese term for "evolution" and for "sympathy".

**Answer:** Evolution: 天演 (Yan Fu), 進化 (Japanese). Sympathy: 善相感 (Yan Fu), 同情 (Japanese).

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 好奇者濫用之泥古者唾棄之二者皆非也

**Answer:** 好奇者濫用之，泥古者唾棄之，二者皆非也。

### 5. translate

Translate 而日本之學者，既先我而定之矣，則沿而用之，何不可之有？

**Answer:** And since the Japanese scholars have already fixed them before us, what is wrong with adopting and using them?
