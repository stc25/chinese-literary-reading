## Introduction

Xue Fucheng, whose diary you read in [Time, aspect and modality](../u07-time-aspect-modality/unit.md), served
as Chinese minister to Britain, France, Italy and Belgium from 1890 to 1894. His diary was
published and widely read as a guide to the West. It is full of **transliterated names**:
places, people, institutions, written in characters chosen for their sound.

The first entry records a journey from the Chinese legation in Paris to the legation in London.
The second is Xue's own attempt to explain how Western names work, and how they are written in
Chinese. Read both with a map and a list of the British ministers to China at hand. Xue is not
always right.

## Text

五月初二日記

巳正，由法館乘馬車至火車棧，換坐輪車。午初三刻，抵阿明，停二十分鐘。未正，抵布倫海口，亦有法國海關，街市繁盛。上輪船，未正一刻啟椗，渡海面二十八洋里。申正一刻，抵英國福克司登海口，又有英國海關。乘火車，開輪，酉正一刻抵采令克路司（即車林克洛司）車棧。換馬車，酉正二刻至使館。計由法館至布倫海口，皆法界，陸程二百五十四洋里。由福克司登至英館，皆英界，陸程七十一洋里。

六月初四日記

西人多以姓氏行，余略誌之矣。亦有以姓繫名後者，如前明萬曆年間意大里國人利瑪竇，以傳教至中國。西人之顯名於中國者，自利瑪竇始。按瑪竇，其姓也；利稽，其名也。彼於其名去一稽字，而以冠於姓之上；名在前而姓在後，西人用此例者頗多。又如英國駐華公使，曰卜魯士，曰阿禮國，曰華爾身，皆其姓也。額爾金世襲伯爵，亦姓卜魯士，額爾金則其所封爵之地名也。若威妥瑪，則威其姓，妥瑪其名；巴夏禮，則巴其姓，夏禮其名。然威、巴二使之姓，亦各有三字；彼但用其首一字，而割去其下兩字也。若德使巴蘭德、法使李梅，則皆其姓。二使在中國，又以上一字作姓，下兩字一字作名矣。

## Translation

Diary for the 2nd day of the 5th month.

At 10 a.m. I went by carriage from the legation in France to the railway station and changed to the train. At 11.45 we reached Amiens and stopped for twenty minutes. At 2 p.m. we reached the port of Boulogne, where there is also a French customs house; the streets are busy and prosperous. We boarded the steamship, which weighed anchor at 2.15 and crossed twenty-eight miles of sea. At 4.15 we reached the English port of Folkestone, where there is an English customs house. We took the train, which started off, and at 6.15 reached Charing Cross station. Changing to a carriage, we reached the legation at 6.30. Reckoning it up: from the legation in France to the port of Boulogne, all French territory, is 254 (kilometres) overland; from Folkestone to the legation in Britain, all British territory, is 71 miles overland.

Diary for the 4th day of the 6th month.

Westerners mostly go by their surnames, as I have briefly noted. There are also those who put the surname after the given name, such as the Italian Ricci (利瑪竇), who came to China as a missionary in the Wanli reign of the former Ming. Of Westerners famous in China, Ricci was the first. Note that 瑪竇 is his surname and 利稽 his given name; he took off the character 稽 from his given name and put it at the head of his surname. Given name first and surname after: many Westerners follow this pattern. Again, the British ministers to China called 卜魯士, 阿禮國 and 華爾身: these are all their surnames. The hereditary Earl of Elgin (額爾金) was also surnamed Bruce (卜魯士); Elgin was the place name of his title. As for 威妥瑪, 威 is his surname and 妥瑪 his given name; for 巴夏禮, 巴 is his surname and 夏禮 his given name. But the surnames of these two ministers, Wei and Ba, each have three characters; they used only the first and cut off the other two. As for the German minister 巴蘭德 and the French minister 李梅, these are both surnames; in China the two ministers took the first character as a surname and the other two characters, or the one character, as a given name.

## Notes

**Identifying transliterations.** Read the characters for their sound, not their meaning. 阿明
Āmíng is Amiens; 布倫 Bùlún is Boulogne; 福克司登 Fúkèsīdēng is Folkestone. Xue gives two
spellings of Charing Cross (采令克路司 and 車林克洛司): there was no fixed system, and the same
place could be spelled in many ways, often reflecting southern pronunciations. See
[Transliteration of foreign names](../../grammar/transliteration.md).

**Telling a name from a word.** A run of characters that makes no sense, especially with common
sound-characters (克, 司, 斯, 爾, 阿, 巴, 德, 里, 利), is probably a transliteration. Context
helps: 抵…海口 "arrived at the port of …" tells you the next word is a place.

**Chinese names for Westerners.** Westerners in China took Chinese names: 威妥瑪 for Thomas Wade,
巴夏禮 for Harry Parkes, 利瑪竇 for Matteo Ricci. The first character usually stands for the
surname (威 Wade, 巴 Parkes, 利 Ricci) and the rest for the given name (妥瑪 Thomas, 夏禮 Harry,
瑪竇 Matteo), in Chinese order. Others kept a full transliteration of the surname (卜魯士 Bruce, 阿禮國 Alcock).

**Xue is wrong about Ricci.** Xue says 瑪竇 is Ricci's surname and 利稽 his given name. It is the
other way round: Ricci (利) is the surname and Matteo (瑪竇) the given name, written in Chinese
order. The diary of an experienced diplomat can still be mistaken; check names against a reference.

**Telling the time.** The day was divided into twelve double hours named by the earthly branches
(子, 丑, 寅 … 巳, 午, 未, 申, 酉 …). 初 is the first hour of each, 正 the second, and 刻 a quarter
of an hour: 午初三刻 is 11.45 a.m., 酉正二刻 6.30 p.m. See
[Dates and calendars](../../toolkit/t02-dates-and-calendars.md).

**洋里.** "Foreign li" was used for both the kilometre and the mile. The 254 洋里 from Paris to
Boulogne fit kilometres; the 71 洋里 from Folkestone to London fit miles. Check units against geography.

**New things, new words.** 火車 "fire-carriage" (train), 輪船 "wheel-ship" (steamship), 輪車
(train), 分鐘 (minute), 海關 (customs): the vocabulary of travel was itself new.
