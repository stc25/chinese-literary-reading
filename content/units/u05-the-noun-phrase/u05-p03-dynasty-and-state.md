## Introduction

Later in the essay Liang makes his central argument: China only *seems* old, because people
mistake the ruling dynasty for the country. A dynasty belongs to one family; a state
belongs to its people. This distinction between 朝 "dynasty" and 國 "state" was new and
important in political writing around 1900.

## Text

夫所謂唐、虞、夏、商、周、秦、漢、魏、晉、宋、齊、梁、陳、隋、唐、宋、元、明、清者，則皆朝名耳。朝也者，一家之私產也。國也者，人民之公產也。朝有朝之老少，國有國之老少。朝與國既異物，則不能以朝之老少而指爲國之老少明矣。

## Translation

Now, what are called Tang, Yu, Xia, Shang, Zhou, Qin, Han, Wei, Jin, Song, Qi, Liang, Chen, Sui, Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming and Qing are all merely the names of dynasties. A dynasty is the private property of one family; a state is the public property of the people. Dynasties have their old age and youth, and states have theirs. Since a dynasty and a state are different things, it is clear that one cannot take the age of a dynasty and declare it to be the age of the state.

## Notes

**也者 marking a topic.** 朝也者，一家之私產也 follows the pattern "A 也者，B 也": "as for A,
it is B". 也者 is a stronger version of the topic marker 也 (see
[也 after a topic](../../grammar/ye-topic.md)). Liang uses the same pattern twice to set
朝 and 國 side by side.

**之 joining modifier and noun.** Every 之 in this passage joins a modifier to a noun:
一家之私產 "the private property of one family", 人民之公產 "the public property of the
people", 朝之老少 "the old age and youth of a dynasty". In 朝有朝之老少, the first 朝 is
the subject and the second modifies 老少: "a dynasty has a dynasty's old age and youth".

**The same character twice.** 唐 and 宋 each appear twice in the list, with different
referents. The first 唐 is the legendary realm of Yao, the second the Tang dynasty;
the first 宋 is the Liu Song dynasty, the second the Song dynasty. The gloss table uses
`#1` and `#2` to tell them apart. The list follows the traditional sequence of
dynasties but leaves out some periods, such as the Five Dynasties (907–960).

**Grammar from later units.** 所謂…者 (unit 8), 則 and 既 (units 7 and 9) and the final 矣 are
glossed here and taught later.

## Exercises

### 1. choice

In 朝**也者**，一家之私產也, what does 也者 do?

- [ ] It ends the sentence
- [x] It marks 朝 as the topic: "as for a dynasty…"
- [ ] It means "the one who"

**Explanation:** "A 也者，B 也" is a topic–comment pattern: "as for A, it is B".

### 2. choice

What does 一家**之**私產 mean?

- [x] the private property of one family
- [ ] one family's being private property
- [ ] one family owns it as private property

**Explanation:** 私產 is a noun and ends the phrase, so 之 joins the modifier 一家 to it.

### 3. short-answer

Why do 唐 and 宋 each appear twice in Liang's list?

**Answer:** They refer to different realms. The first 唐 is Yao's legendary realm and the second the Tang dynasty (618–907); the first 宋 is the Liu Song (420–479) and the second the Song dynasty (960–1279).

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate the two sentences.

**Text:** 朝也者一家之私產也國也者人民之公產也

**Answer:** 朝也者，一家之私產也。國也者，人民之公產也。

**Explanation:** 也者 closes a topic, so a comma follows; the final 也 ends each sentence.

### 5. translate

Translate 朝有朝之老少，國有國之老少。

**Answer:** Dynasties have their old age and youth, and states have theirs.

**Explanation:** Literally: "A dynasty has a dynasty's old age and youth; a state has a state's old age and youth."
