## Introduction

Liang Qichao 梁啟超 (1873–1929) was the most widely read writer of the late Qing.
After the failure of the 1898 reforms he fled to Japan, where he wrote 〈少年中國說〉
("On Young China", 1900). The essay opens by answering a description of China that
Liang heard in Japan. Liang refers to himself here by his style name, 任公 Rengong.

## Text

日本人之稱我中國也，一則曰老大帝國，再則曰老大帝國。是語也，盖襲譯歐西人之言也。嗚呼！我中國其果老大矣乎？任公曰：惡！是何言！是何言！吾心目中有一少年中國在！

## Translation

When the Japanese speak of our China, they call it "the old empire", and again "the old empire". This expression, it seems, is a borrowed translation of what Europeans say. Alas! Has our China really grown old? Rengong says: Nonsense! What talk is this! What talk is this! In my mind's eye there is a young China!

## Notes

**Two kinds of 之.** The passage has two 之, doing different jobs:

- In 日本人**之**稱我中國也, 之 stands between a subject (日本人) and its verb (稱). It turns the
  whole clause "the Japanese call our China" into a noun phrase: "the Japanese calling our
  China". See [之 between subject and verb](../../grammar/zhi-nominaliser.md).
- In 歐西人**之**言, 之 joins a modifier (歐西人 "Europeans") to a noun (言 "words"): "the words
  of Europeans". See [之 joining modifier and noun](../../grammar/zhi-attributive.md).

A quick test: look at what follows 之. If it is a noun that ends the phrase (言), 之 is
joining a modifier to it. If it is a verb with its own object (稱我中國), 之 is turning a clause into a noun phrase.

**Topic and comment.** Both sentences begin with a topic closed by 也
([也 after a topic](../../grammar/ye-topic.md)): 日本人之稱我中國也 "as for the Japanese
speaking of our China" and 是語也 "as for this expression". The comment follows.

**其 is not always "his, its".** In 我中國其果老大矣乎, 其 is not a pronoun. Placed before
the verb in a question, it expresses doubt or disbelief: "can it really be that…?" See [其 expressing doubt](../../grammar/qi-modal.md); the pronoun 其 is taught in
[其: "his, her, its, their"](../../grammar/qi-pronoun.md).

**有…在** "there is … (right here)". Adding 在 at the end stresses that the young China really exists.

## Exercises

### 1. choice

In 日本人**之**稱我中國也, what does 之 do?

- [ ] It marks possession: "the Japanese people's China"
- [x] It makes 日本人稱我中國 into a noun phrase: "the Japanese calling our China"
- [ ] It is an object pronoun: "it"

**Explanation:** 之 stands between a subject (日本人) and its verb (稱), and the whole clause becomes the topic of the sentence.

### 2. choice

In 歐西人**之**言, what does 之 do?

- [x] It joins a modifier to a noun: "the words of Europeans"
- [ ] It makes a clause into a noun phrase: "Europeans speaking"
- [ ] It is an object pronoun: "Europeans say it"

**Explanation:** 言 here is a noun, "words", and it ends the phrase. 歐西人 modifies it.

### 3. choice

In 我中國**其**果老大矣乎, what does 其 do?

- [ ] It means "its", referring back to China
- [ ] It means "his", referring to Liang
- [x] It expresses doubt: "can it really be that…?"

**Explanation:** 其 here stands before the verb phrase in a question and adds doubt or disbelief. It does not refer to anything.

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 是語也盖襲譯歐西人之言也

**Answer:** 是語也，盖襲譯歐西人之言也。

**Explanation:** 也 after 是語 closes the topic, so a comma follows. The second 也 ends the sentence.

### 5. translate

Translate 日本人之稱我中國也，一則曰老大帝國，再則曰老大帝國。

**Answer:** When the Japanese speak of our China, they call it "the old empire", and again "the old empire".

**Explanation:** 曰 here means "to call (it)", not "to say". The repetition stresses how often the label is used.
