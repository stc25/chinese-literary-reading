## Introduction

Before deciding whether China is old or young, Liang compares old people with young
people in a long series of images. Some are traditional (the evening sun, a monk);
others are new and foreign (brandy, the pyramids, the Siberian railway, which was being built at the time).

## Text

老年人如夕照，少年人如朝陽；老年人如瘠牛，少年人如乳虎；老年人如僧，少年人如俠；老年人如字典，少年人如戲文；老年人如鴉片烟，少年人如潑蘭地酒；老年人如別行星之隕石，少年人如大洋海之珊瑚島；老年人如埃及沙漠之金字塔，少年人如西伯利亞之鐵路；老年人如秋後之柳，少年人如春前之草；老年人如死海之瀦爲澤，少年人如長江之初發源：此老年與少年性格不同之大略也。任公曰：人固有之，國亦宜然。

## Translation

The old are like the evening sun, the young like the morning sun; the old are like a lean ox, the young like a tiger cub; the old are like a monk, the young like a knight-errant; the old are like a dictionary, the young like a play; the old are like opium, the young like brandy; the old are like a meteorite from another planet, the young like a coral island in the ocean; the old are like the pyramids of the Egyptian desert, the young like the Siberian railway; the old are like the willow after autumn, the young like the grass before spring; the old are like the Dead Sea that has pooled into a marsh, the young like the Yangzi just rising from its source. This is the general outline of how the old and the young differ in character. Rengong says: it is so with people, and it must be so with states too.

## Notes

**One pattern, many times.** Each pair follows the pattern 老年人如 A，少年人如 B. Once you
have read the first pair, the rest of the passage is mostly new vocabulary.

**之 joining modifier and noun.** Most of the 之 in this passage join a modifier to a noun,
and English usually reverses the order:

- 秋後**之**柳 "after-autumn 之 willow" → "the willow after autumn"
- 埃及沙漠**之**金字塔 "Egypt desert 之 pyramid" → "the pyramids of the Egyptian desert"

The modifier can be a whole clause: in 此老年與少年性格不同**之**大略也, the clause 老年與少年性格不同
"the old and the young differ in character" modifies 大略 "outline". See
[之 joining modifier and noun](../../grammar/zhi-attributive.md).

**之 between subject and verb.** The last pair is different. In 死海**之**瀦爲澤 and
長江**之**初發源, the words after 之 are verbs (瀦爲澤 "pool into a marsh", 初發源
"just rise from the source"). 之 turns "the Dead Sea pools into a marsh" into a noun phrase,
which is the object of 如. See [之 between subject and verb](../../grammar/zhi-nominaliser.md).

**之 as a pronoun.** In 人固有之, 之 comes after the verb 有 and means "it, this": a third use, taught in
[之 as object pronoun](../../grammar/zhi-object.md).

**Foreign words.** 潑蘭地, 埃及 and 西伯利亞 are transliterations: the characters are chosen
for their sound, not their meaning. 金字塔 "金-character tower" describes the pyramid's
shape, which resembles the character 金. See [Transliteration of foreign names](../../grammar/transliteration.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

In which phrase does 之 stand between a subject and its verb?

- [ ] 秋後之柳
- [ ] 埃及沙漠之金字塔
- [x] 死海之瀦爲澤
- [ ] 西伯利亞之鐵路

**Explanation:** After 之 in 死海之瀦爲澤 comes a verb phrase (瀦爲澤 "pool into a marsh"). In the other phrases a noun follows 之 and ends the phrase.

### 2. choice

In 人固有**之**, what does 之 mean?

- [ ] "of": it joins a modifier to a noun
- [x] "it, this": it is the object of 有
- [ ] It makes 人固有 into a noun phrase

**Explanation:** 之 comes straight after the verb 有 and nothing follows it, so it is the object pronoun.

### 3. short-answer

Which of Liang's images use foreign things? Name at least three.

**Answer:** 潑蘭地酒 (brandy), 埃及沙漠之金字塔 (the pyramids of Egypt), 西伯利亞之鐵路 (the Siberian railway), 死海 (the Dead Sea).

**Explanation:** Mixing traditional and foreign images was typical of Liang's new essay style. Unit 16 returns to it.

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 老年人如秋後之柳少年人如春前之草

**Answer:** 老年人如秋後之柳，少年人如春前之草。

### 5. translate

Translate 此老年與少年性格不同之大略也。

**Answer:** This is the general outline of how the old and the young differ in character.

**Explanation:** The clause 老年與少年性格不同 modifies 大略 through 之. 也 ends the statement "this is…".
