## Introduction

Zhang Zhidong 張之洞 (1837–1909), governor-general of Hubei and Hunan, wrote 《勸學篇》
("Exhortation to Learning") in 1898 to steer the reform movement: Chinese learning as the
foundation, Western learning for practical use. The court distributed it throughout the empire.

This passage opens in the **unpunctuated** view. Try to divide it yourself, using the
strategies in [Dividing unpunctuated text (句讀)](../../grammar/judou.md), before switching to the
modern view. The opening alludes to the 左傳 (Zuo Commentary): you do not need the
background to find the breaks.

## Text

昔楚莊王之霸也，以民生在勤箴其民，以日討軍實儆其軍，以禍至無日訓其國人。夫楚當春秋魯文、宣之際，土方闢，兵方強，國勢方張，齊、晉、秦、宋無敢抗顏行，誰能禍楚者，何為而急迫震懼如是之皇皇耶？君子曰：「不知其禍則辱至矣，知其禍則福至矣。」今日之世變，豈特春秋所未有，抑秦、漢以至元、明所未有也，語其禍，則共工之狂、辛有之痛，不足喻也。

## Translation

In the past, when King Zhuang of Chu was hegemon, he admonished his people with "the people's livelihood lies in diligence", warned his army by inspecting its equipment every day, and instructed the people of his state with "disaster may come any day". Now Chu, at the time of Dukes Wen and Xuan of Lu in the Spring and Autumn period, was just expanding its territory; its army was just growing strong and its power just extending; none of Qi, Jin, Qin or Song dared stand up to it. Who could bring disaster on Chu? Why then was he so anxious, fearful and agitated? The gentleman says: "If you do not know your danger, disgrace will come; if you know your danger, good fortune will come." The changes in the world today have no precedent, not merely in the Spring and Autumn period, but from Qin and Han down to Yuan and Ming. To speak of the danger: even the madness of Gonggong or the grief of Xin You is not enough to illustrate it.

## Notes

**How the breaks can be found.** Every break in this passage can be found from the text
itself:

| Signal | Example | Break |
|---|---|---|
| 也 closing a topic | 昔楚莊王之霸也 | comma after 也 |
| parallel phrases | 以…箴其民，以…儆其軍，以…訓其國人 | a break before each 以 |
| 夫 opening a new point | 夫楚當春秋… | full stop before 夫 |
| parallel short clauses | 土方闢，兵方強，國勢方張 | break after each 方 + verb |
| question words and 耶 | 誰能禍楚者，何為而…耶 | question mark after 耶 |
| 曰 + quotation | 君子曰：「…」 | colon after 曰 |
| 矣 | …辱至矣，…福至矣 | break after each 矣 |
| 也 at the end of a statement | …所未有也 / …不足喻也 | full stop after 也 |

See [Dividing unpunctuated text](../../grammar/judou.md) and [Particles that mark boundaries](../../grammar/boundary-particles.md).

**Lists of names.** 齊晉秦宋 and 秦漢以至元明 are lists of states and dynasties: the edition
separates them with 、. When a run of single characters makes no sense as a sentence, check whether it is a list of names.

**Allusions.** Zhang's opening retells a passage of the 左傳 about King Zhuang of Chu, and
共工 and 辛有 are figures from ancient myth and history. The argument does not depend on
knowing them: the structure "even X is not enough to illustrate it" tells you they stand for extreme disaster.
See [Allusion and quotation](../../grammar/allusion.md).

## Exercises

### 1. punctuate

Punctuate the opening sentence.

**Text:** 昔楚莊王之霸也以民生在勤箴其民以日討軍實儆其軍以禍至無日訓其國人

**Answer:** 昔楚莊王之霸也，以民生在勤箴其民，以日討軍實儆其軍，以禍至無日訓其國人。

**Explanation:** 也 closes the topic; the three parallel 以-phrases each end with 其 + noun.

### 2. punctuate

Punctuate the description of Chu.

**Text:** 土方闢兵方強國勢方張

**Answer:** 土方闢，兵方強，國勢方張，

**Explanation:** Three parallel clauses, each with 方 + verb. The sentence continues, so the last mark is a comma.

### 3. punctuate

Punctuate the quotation.

**Text:** 君子曰不知其禍則辱至矣知其禍則福至矣

**Answer:** 君子曰：「不知其禍則辱至矣，知其禍則福至矣。」

**Explanation:** 曰 introduces a quotation; 矣 ends each of its two parallel halves.

### 4. choice

Which word tells you that a new point begins at 夫楚當春秋…?

- [x] 夫, which opens a general statement or new point
- [ ] 楚, the name of the state
- [ ] 當, "at the time of"

### 5. short-answer

What is the point of Zhang's comparison with King Zhuang of Chu?

**Answer:** Even a ruler at the height of his power warned his people that disaster could come any day; China today faces far greater danger than any in the past, so it has even more reason to be vigilant.
