## Introduction

Two more articles of the Treaty of Nanjing (1842), which you read in
[Prepositions](../u06-prepositions/unit.md). This passage opens in the **vertical,
unpunctuated** view, close to how the treaty was printed. Look for the **gaps** in the
columns. Two of them are marks of respect (抬頭 "elevation"); one is not.

Article 9 grants an amnesty to Chinese who had worked with the British. Article 10 deals
with tariffs: the rate was famously left blank in the treaty and fixed later.

## Text

一、凡係中國人，前在英人所㨿之邑居住者，與英人有來往者，或有跟隨及俟候英國官人者，均由大皇帝俯降御㫖，謄錄天下，恩准免罪；凡係中國人，為英國事被拿監禁受難者，亦加恩釋放。

一、前第二條內言明開關俾英國商民居住通商之廣州等五處，應納進口、出口貨稅、餉費，均宜秉公議定則例，由部頒發曉示，以便英商按例交納；今又議定，英國貨物自在某港按例納稅後，即准由中國商人遍運天下，而路所經過稅關不得加重稅例，只可按估價則例若干，每兩加稅不過分。

## Translation

Item. All Chinese who formerly lived in towns occupied by the British, who had dealings with the British, or who served or waited on British officials, shall be pardoned by an edict graciously issued by the Great Emperor and copied out throughout the empire; and all Chinese who were arrested, imprisoned and suffered because of British affairs shall likewise be graciously released.

Item. At the five ports, Canton and the others, which were opened by article 2 for British merchants to live and trade, the import and export duties and charges to be paid shall be fairly agreed as a tariff and issued and made public by the Board, so that British merchants can pay according to the tariff. It is further agreed that once British goods have paid duty according to the tariff at a port, they may be transported throughout the empire by Chinese merchants, and the customs stations along the way may not increase the rates, but may only add, on the valuation, not more than … fen per tael.

## Notes

**Elevation (抬頭).** In official documents, words for the emperor, his acts and his
institutions were given space as a mark of respect. In vertical text the word could start a
new column, be raised above the others (單抬, 雙抬, 三抬: one, two or three characters
higher), or follow a blank space (空抬). The transcription shows two such gaps:

- **　恩准免罪**: before 恩准 "graciously grants", an act of the emperor;
- **由　部頒發**: before 部 "the Board", an imperial ministry.

See [Elevation (抬頭) and spacing](../../grammar/elevation.md).

**A gap that is not elevation.** **每兩加稅不過　分** "not more than __ fen per tael": here the
blank is a figure **left to be filled in**. The transit duty was not agreed at Nanjing.
Before reading a gap as a mark of respect, ask whether the word after it deserves one.

**Elevation is a reading aid.** In unpunctuated text, an elevated word usually begins a new
phrase, so a gap is also a likely place for a break. But the modern punctuation does not
show elevation at all, which is one reason to look at the original layout.

**Long sentences.** Each article here is a single long sentence. Read it as a series of
clauses: who (凡係中國人…者), what is done (均由大皇帝…恩准免罪), then a second group (凡係中國人…亦加恩釋放).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

In the original layout, why is there a space before 恩准?

- [ ] It marks the end of a sentence.
- [x] 恩准 is an act of the emperor, and the space is a mark of respect.
- [ ] A word is missing.

### 2. choice

What is the gap in 每兩加稅不過　分?

- [ ] a mark of respect for the tariff
- [x] a blank where the rate was to be filled in later
- [ ] a sentence break

### 3. punctuate

Punctuate the clause.

**Text:** 均由大皇帝俯降御㫖謄錄天下恩准免罪

**Answer:** 均由大皇帝俯降御㫖，謄錄天下，恩准免罪；

**Explanation:** Three steps: issuing the edict, copying it out, granting the pardon. The article continues, so the clause ends with a semicolon.

### 4. short-answer

Who is covered by the amnesty in article 9?

**Answer:** Chinese who had lived in British-occupied towns, had dealings with the British or served British officials; and Chinese arrested and imprisoned because of British affairs.
