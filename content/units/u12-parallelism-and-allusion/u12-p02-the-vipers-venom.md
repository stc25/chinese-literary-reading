## Introduction

Lin Shu knew no foreign language. Working with collaborators who read the originals aloud
to him, he turned more than 180 Western novels into elegant literary Chinese. In 1901, with
Wei Yi 魏易, he translated Harriet Beecher Stowe's *Uncle Tom's Cabin* as 《黑奴籲天錄》
"Record of the Black Slaves' Appeal to Heaven".

In his preface he explains why: the treatment of Black slaves in America, he argues, is now
being turned against Chinese workers there. (The United States had barred Chinese labourers
by the Exclusion Act of 1882.) Notice how he argues: through an image, through matched
phrases, and through a word borrowed from the law courts.

## Text

華盛頓以大公之心，官其國不為私產，而仍不能弛奴禁，必待林肯，奴籍始幸脫。邇又寢遷其處黑奴者，以處黃人矣。

夫蝮之不竟伸其毒，必別齧草木舒憤；後人來觸死莖，亦靡不死。吾黃人殆觸其死莖乎？

國蓄地產而不發，民生貧薄不可自聊，始以工食於美洲，歲致羨其家。彼中精計學者，患洩其銀幣，乃酷待華工，以絕其來。因之黃人受虐，或加甚於黑人。而國力既弱，為使者復餒懾不敢與爭，又無通人紀載其事，余無從知之。而可據為前讞者，特《黑奴籲天錄》耳。

方今囂訟者，已膠固不可喻譬；而傾心彼族者，又誤信西人寬待其藩屬，躍躍然欲趨而附之。則吾之書足以儆醒之者，寧可少哉！

## Translation

Washington, with a spirit of great public-mindedness, made his country a public trust and not his private property, and yet he still could not lift the slave laws. It had to wait for Lincoln before the slaves were fortunately freed from their bondage. Recently, moreover, the treatment once given to Black slaves has gradually been shifted onto the yellow people.

Now, when a viper cannot fully discharge its venom, it will bite grass and trees instead to vent its anger; and people who later touch the dead stalks die without exception. Are we yellow people perhaps touching its dead stalks?

Our country stores up the products of its land and does not develop them. The people's livelihood is poor and meagre and they cannot support themselves, so they began to make their living by labour in America, sending their savings home every year. Their economists, worried that their silver was draining away, treated Chinese labourers cruelly in order to stop them coming. As a result the yellow people suffer abuse that is perhaps worse than the Black people's. And since our national power is weak, our envoys are moreover too timid and fearful to contest it, and no learned man has recorded these events, so I have had no way of knowing about them. The only precedent I can rely on is *Uncle Tom's Cabin*.

At present, those who clamour [against the foreigners] are already too set in their ways to be persuaded by example; and those who give their hearts to that race wrongly believe that Westerners treat their dependencies leniently, and are eager to rush and attach themselves to them. So how can a book of mine that is enough to warn and awaken them be dispensed with!

## Notes

**An image as argument.** The viper that bites plants when it cannot bite a person, and the
plants that then poison whoever touches them: Lin Shu builds a chain from American slavery
to the Chinese in America. Slavery has been abolished, but its poison remains, and the
Chinese are now the ones who touch it. The rhetorical question 吾黃人殆觸其死莖乎 asks the
reader to draw the conclusion.

**A word from the law courts.** 讞 is a legal verdict, and 前讞 a precedent. Calling the novel
a 前讞 makes it a case already judged, from which the Chinese case can be argued. Lin Shu
borrows the vocabulary of one field (law) to make a point in another (literature).

**Parallelism.** The last paragraph sets two groups side by side:

| who | already/also | their error |
|---|---|---|
| 囂訟者 | 已 | 膠固不可喻譬 |
| 傾心彼族者 | 又 | 誤信西人寬待其藩屬 |

The two halves are not the same length, but they are built alike (… 者，已/又 …), and the
semicolon divides them. Parallel construction is often looser in prose than in verse: look
for matching **structure** rather than matching length. Shorter pairs: 國蓄地產而不發，民生貧薄不可自聊.

**The language of race.** 黃人, 黃種 "the yellow race" and 彼族 "that race" reflect ideas of
racial struggle widespread around 1900, partly through Yan Fu's account of evolution (see
[The struggle for existence](../u09-linking-clauses/u09-p03-the-struggle-for-existence.md)).
Lin Shu's sympathy for the slaves is sincere, but he reads their story as a warning to his own
"race". Note the period terminology without adopting it.

**囂訟者 and 傾心彼族者.** The preface was written in 1901, just after the Boxer uprising. The
first group is probably the anti-foreign party; the second, those who looked to the Western
powers for protection. Lin Shu rejects both.

**計學.** 計學 "economics" was Yan Fu's coinage (in his translation of Adam Smith); it was
later replaced by the Japanese-derived 經濟學. See [Neologisms](../../grammar/neologisms.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

In the viper image, who are the people who "touch the dead stalks"?

- [ ] the Black slaves in America
- [x] the Chinese, who now suffer the treatment once given to slaves
- [ ] the American economists

### 2. choice

Why does Lin Shu call the novel a 前讞?

- [ ] because it is about a trial
- [x] because it is a "case already judged" that can serve as a precedent for the Chinese case
- [ ] because the translators were lawyers

### 3. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 國蓄地產而不發民生貧薄不可自聊

**Answer:** 國蓄地產而不發，民生貧薄不可自聊，

**Explanation:** Two parallel clauses: the country (國) and the people (民生), each with a negative ending.

### 4. short-answer

Which two groups does Lin Shu criticise in the last paragraph, and what is wrong with each?

**Answer:** Those who clamour against foreigners (囂訟者), who are too stubborn to be persuaded; and those who admire the Westerners (傾心彼族者), who wrongly believe the West treats its dependencies kindly.

### 5. translate

Translate 吾黃人殆觸其死莖乎？

**Answer:** Are we yellow people perhaps touching its dead stalks?
