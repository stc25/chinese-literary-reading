## Introduction

Wang Guowei was a scholar of philosophy, literature, history and ancient inscriptions. His
《人間詞話》 "Remarks on Song Lyrics from the Human World", first published in 1908 in the journal
國粹學報, is a set of short entries on the song lyric (詞 cí), the sung poetry that reached its
height in the Five Dynasties and Song. His key term is 境界 jìngjiè "world, realm": the scene
and feeling a poem creates.

The entries are made of **quotations**. Wang quotes lines of poetry and expects you to know
the poems they come from. In the most famous entry he takes three love lyrics and makes them
describe something quite different.

## Text

詞以境界爲最上。有境界則自成髙格，自有名句。五代北宋之詞所以獨絶者在此。

古今之成大事業、大學問者，必經過三種之境界：「昨夜西風凋碧樹，獨上髙樓，望盡天涯路。」此第一境也。「衣帶漸寬終不悔，爲伊消得人憔悴。」此第二境也。「眾裡尋他千百度，驀然回首，那人卻在，燈火闌珊處。」此第三境也。此等語皆非大詞人不能道。然遽以此意解釋諸詞，恐爲晏歐諸公所不許也。

「西風吹渭水，落日滿長安。」美成以之入詞，白仁甫以之入曲，此借古人之境界爲我之境界者也。然非自有境界，古人亦不爲我用。

## Translation

The song lyric is at its highest when it has a world of its own. If it has one, it naturally achieves a lofty style and naturally has famous lines. That is why the lyrics of the Five Dynasties and the Northern Song are unrivalled.

All who have achieved great undertakings or great learning, past and present, have passed through three stages. "Last night the west wind withered the green trees; alone I climb the high tower and gaze to the end of the road at the edge of the sky": this is the first stage. "My sash grows looser, but I never regret it; for her I waste away": this is the second stage. "In the crowd I sought her a thousand times; suddenly I turn my head, and there she is, where the lamplight is dim": this is the third stage. Only great lyricists could have written words like these. But to interpret those lyrics in this sense straight away would, I fear, not be accepted by Yan, Ouyang and the others.

"The west wind blows over the Wei river; the setting sun fills Chang'an." Zhou Bangyan put this into a lyric, and Bai Pu into an aria: this is borrowing the world of an earlier writer to make one's own. But unless one has a world of one's own, the ancients will not be of use to one either.

## Literal translation

Lyric taking world as highest. Have world then of-itself achieve lofty style, of-itself have famous lines. Five Dynasties Northern Song's lyrics that-by-which unrivalled thing lies here.

Past-present's achieve great undertaking, great learning ones, must pass-through three kinds' stages: "Last-night west-wind wither green trees, alone climb high tower, gaze-to-end sky-edge road." This first stage. "Clothes-sash gradually loose, finally not regret, for her waste-away person haggard." This second stage. "Crowd-in seek her thousand hundred times, suddenly turn-head, that person but is-at, lamp-fire dim place." This third stage. These-kind words all not-great-lyricist cannot say. But rashly with this meaning explain the lyrics, fear by Yan Ou all-lords what-not-accepted.

"West wind blows Wei water, setting sun fills Chang'an." Meicheng with-it entered lyric, Bai Renfu with-it entered aria, this borrow ancients' world make my world ones. But not of-itself have world, ancients also not by me used.

## Notes

**Tracing the three quotations.**

| Stage | Lines | Poem |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 昨夜西風凋碧樹，獨上髙樓，望盡天涯路 | 晏殊 Yan Shu, 《蝶戀花》 |
| 2 | 衣帶漸寬終不悔，爲伊消得人憔悴 | 柳永 Liu Yong, 《鳳棲梧》 (in Wang's time often attributed to 歐陽修 Ouyang Xiu) |
| 3 | 眾裡尋他千百度，驀然回首，那人卻在，燈火闌珊處 | 辛棄疾 Xin Qiji, 《青玉案・元夕》 |

All three are love poems: longing for someone far away, wasting away for love, finding the
beloved at last in the lantern-festival crowd. Wang reads them as the stages of a scholar's
search: setting a distant goal, labouring without regret, and sudden discovery. Taking lines
out of their context to give them a new meaning is called 斷章取義 duànzhāng qǔyì. Wang admits
it: the poets themselves "would not accept" his reading.

**A misquotation.** The lines in entry 17 come from 賈島 Jia Dao's poem 《憶江上吳處士》,
where they read 秋風吹渭水，落葉滿長安 "the **autumn** wind blows over the Wei, **fallen leaves**
fill Chang'an". Wang's version, with 西風 and 落日, is closer to the way the later writers
reused the lines (Bai Pu has 西風渭水，落日長安). Writers quoted from memory, and an
allusion may come through later borrowings rather than from the original. Always check.

**Borrowing a world.** Entry 17 is itself about allusion: 借古人之境界爲我之境界 "borrowing
the world of an earlier writer to make one's own". Wang's condition, 非自有境界，古人亦不爲我用,
is a double negative: only if you have a world of your own can earlier writers serve you.

**非…不能.** 此等語皆非大詞人不能道 "words like these, no one but a great lyricist could say"
is the same pattern: "not X, cannot" = "only X can".

**Names.** Wang refers to poets by courtesy names (美成 for 周邦彥, 仁甫 for 白樸) or by
surname alone (晏歐 for 晏殊 and 歐陽修, using the first character of the double surname
歐陽). See [Names, titles and offices](../../toolkit/t03-names-titles-offices.md).

**Parallelism inside the sentence.** 有境界則自成髙格，自有名句 pairs 自成髙格 and 自有名句;
美成以之入詞，白仁甫以之入曲 pairs two writers and two genres (詞, 曲). The repetition
此第一境也 … 此第二境也 … 此第三境也 frames the three quotations.

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What are the three quoted lyrics about in their original poems?

- [ ] the stages of scholarship
- [x] love and longing
- [ ] the fall of the Song dynasty

### 2. short-answer

Why does Wang say that Yan Shu, Ouyang Xiu and the others "would not accept" his reading?

**Answer:** Because he gives their love poems a meaning they did not intend: the stages of achieving great work or learning (斷章取義).

### 3. short-answer

How does Wang's quotation 西風吹渭水，落日滿長安 differ from Jia Dao's original line?

**Answer:** Jia Dao wrote 秋風吹渭水，落葉滿長安: "autumn wind" (秋風) for "west wind", and "fallen leaves" (落葉) for "setting sun".

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 美成以之入詞白仁甫以之入曲此借古人之境界爲我之境界者也

**Answer:** 美成以之入詞，白仁甫以之入曲，此借古人之境界爲我之境界者也。

**Explanation:** The two parallel clauses (…以之入詞 / …以之入曲) are followed by the judgement, closed by 者也.

### 5. translate

Translate 然非自有境界，古人亦不爲我用。

**Answer:** But unless one has a world of one's own, the ancients will not be of use to one either.
