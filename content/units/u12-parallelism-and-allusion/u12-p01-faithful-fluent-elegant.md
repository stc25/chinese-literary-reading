## Introduction

Yan Fu's translation of T. H. Huxley's *Evolution and Ethics* (1893), published as 《天演論》,
introduced the idea of evolution to Chinese readers (you read from his essay 原強 in
[Linking clauses](../u09-linking-clauses/unit.md)). It opens with a list of "general remarks on
translation" (譯例言), each item beginning 一、. The first sets out the three difficulties of
translation, 信、達、雅, a formula still quoted in China today.

Yan Fu justifies the formula with three quotations from the Classics, and expects you to
recognise them. This passage is about **allusion**: how a writer builds an argument out of
borrowed words.

## Text

一、譯事三難：信、達、雅。求其信已大難矣，顧信矣不達，雖譯猶不譯也，則達尚焉。海通已來，象寄之才，隨地多有，而任取一書，責其與於斯二者則已寡矣。其故在淺嘗，一也；偏至，二也；辨之者少，三也。

一、《易》曰：「修辭立誠。」子曰：「辭達而已。」又曰：「言之無文，行之不遠。」三曰乃文章正軌，亦即為譯事楷模。故信達而外，求其爾雅，此不僅期以行遠已耳。實則精理微言，用漢以前字法、句法，則為達易；用近世利俗文字，則求達難。

## Translation

Item. Translation has three difficulties: faithfulness, comprehensibility and elegance. To be faithful is already very hard; but if a translation is faithful and not comprehensible, then although it is translated it is as if it were not. So comprehensibility comes first. Since sea communications were opened, there are interpreters everywhere; but take any book at random and ask it to measure up to these two standards, and few do. There are three reasons: first, superficial learning; second, one-sidedness; third, few people can tell the difference.

Item. The *Changes* says: "In refining your words, establish sincerity." The Master said: "Words should get the meaning across, that is all." And: "If speech has no elegance, it will not travel far." These three sayings are the proper course for writing, and so also the model for translation. So beyond faithfulness and comprehensibility I aim at elegance, and not only in the hope that the translation will travel far. In fact, with subtle principles and profound words, using the vocabulary and syntax of before the Han makes them easy to get across; using modern popular language makes it hard.

## Literal translation

One: translation-business three difficulties: faithfulness, comprehensibility, elegance. Seek its faithfulness already greatly hard; but faithful and not comprehensible, although translated still not translated, then comprehensibility is-valued. Sea-communication since, elephant-lodge's talents, everywhere many there-are, but at-random take one book, require it attain these two, then already few. Its reason lies-in shallow-tasting, one; one-sided-reaching, two; discern-it-ones few, three.

One: *Changes* says: "Refine words establish sincerity." The Master said: "Words get-across, that is all." Again said: "Speak it without pattern, go it not far." Three sayings are writing's proper track, also are translation-business's model. So faithfulness comprehensibility beyond, seek its elegance, this not only hope thereby go far, that is all. In-fact subtle principles fine words, use Han-before word-method, sentence-method, then achieve comprehensibility easy; use recent-times benefit-vulgar writing, then seek comprehensibility hard.

## Notes

**Three quotations.** Yan Fu's argument for 雅 rests on three classical sayings. A reader of
1898 would know them by heart; you have to trace them.

| Quotation | Source | As Yan Fu quotes it |
|---|---|---|
| 修辭立其誠 | 《易》, 乾卦 文言 | 修辭立誠: one character dropped |
| 辭達而已矣 | 《論語》 衛靈公 | 辭達而已: final 矣 dropped |
| 言之無文，行而不遠 | 《左傳》 襄公二十五年, words of Confucius (仲尼曰) | 言之無文，行之不遠 |

Writers often quoted from memory, shortened a line or changed a word. When you trace a
quotation, note the differences: sometimes they are slips, sometimes deliberate. See
[Allusion (典故) and quotation](../../grammar/allusion.md).

**又曰 "and he also said".** Yan Fu introduces the third saying with 又曰, as if it were
Confucius again. It is: the 左傳 quotes it as Confucius' words, though it is not in the 論語.

**Picking up the quotation.** 期以行遠 "in the hope that it will travel far" takes 行遠 from
言之無文，行之不遠. An allusion can echo inside the author's own sentence, not just in quotation marks.

**象寄之才 "interpreters".** The 禮記 (王制) names the interpreters for the four directions:
東方曰寄，南方曰象，西方曰狄鞮，北方曰譯. Yan Fu's phrase means "people who can translate", but
it also shows his learning, and so his right to judge translators. Only 譯 has survived as the ordinary word.

**爾雅.** As an adjective, 爾雅 means "elegant, correct". It is also the title of the oldest
Chinese dictionary. Yan Fu means the classical language of before the Han, the language the
爾雅 explains.

**Parallelism.** The last sentence is two matching halves:

| | means | | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 用 | 漢以前字法、句法 | 則 | 為達易 |
| 用 | 近世利俗文字 | 則 | 求達難 |

The parallel shows the contrast at once: old language, easy; modern language, hard. See
[Parallelism (對偶)](../../grammar/parallelism.md).

**Four characters.** 精理微言, 象寄之才, 淺嘗, 偏至: Yan Fu's prose moves in short, weighty
units, many of them four characters long. See [Four- and six-character prose](../../grammar/four-character-phrases.md).

**Counting.** 其故在淺嘗，一也；偏至，二也；辨之者少，三也 "the reasons are: superficial
learning, first; one-sidedness, second; few who can judge, third" is a common way of listing
reasons: the item first, the number after, each with 也.

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What does 雖譯猶不譯也 mean?

- [ ] Although it is hard, he still translates it.
- [x] Although it is translated, it is as if it had not been.
- [ ] Even translators do not translate it.

### 2. short-answer

Which of Yan Fu's three quotations is not from the 論語, although he introduces it with 又曰 as Confucius' words?

**Answer:** 言之無文，行之不遠, from the 左傳 (襄公二十五年), where it is attributed to Confucius.

### 3. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 實則精理微言用漢以前字法句法則為達易用近世利俗文字則求達難

**Answer:** 實則精理微言，用漢以前字法、句法，則為達易；用近世利俗文字，則求達難。

**Explanation:** The two halves are parallel: 用…則… / 用…則…. A semicolon separates them.

### 4. translate

Translate 故信達而外，求其爾雅。

**Answer:** So beyond faithfulness and comprehensibility, I aim at elegance.
