## Introduction

By 1919 the "new culture" teachers at Peking University (Chen Duxiu, Hu Shi, Qian Xuantong)
were writing in the vernacular and attacking Confucian ethics. On 18 March 1919 the conservative
newspaper 《公言報》 printed an open letter to the university's chancellor, Cai Yuanpei 蔡元培, from
Lin Shu, the translator you met in [U12](../u12-parallelism-and-allusion/u12-p02-the-vipers-venom.md)
and [U18](../u18-scholarship-and-history/u18-p01-the-biography-of-yan-fu.md). Lin was 67, a Qing
loyalist, and the most famous writer of classical prose alive.

Lin's letter accuses the teachers of two crimes: overthrowing Confucius and the moral bonds, and
"abolishing the old books and writing in the common speech". This passage gives his case against
the vernacular. Notice that he writes it, of course, in polished literary Chinese, framed by the
formulas of a letter between equals.

## Text

鶴卿先生太史足下：

……且天下惟有真學術、真道德，始足獨樹一幟，使人景從。若盡廢古書，行用土語為文字，則都下引車賣漿之徒，所操之語，按之皆有文法，不類閩廣人為無文法之啁啾。據此，則凡京津之稗販，均可用為教授矣。若《水滸》、《紅樓》，皆白話之聖，並足為教科之書，不知《水滸》中辭吻多采岳珂之《金陀萃篇》，《紅樓》亦不止為一人手筆，作者均博極群書之人。總之，非讀破萬卷，不能為古文，亦並不能為白話。……

弟閩人也，南蠻鴃舌，亦願習中原之語言。脫授我者不以中原之語言，仍令我為鴃舌之閩語，可乎？蓋存國粹而授《說文》，可也，以《說文》為客，以白話為主，不可也。……

林紓頓首。

## Translation

To Mr Heqing, Academician:

…… Moreover, only true learning and true morality are enough to raise a banner of one's own and make people follow. If the old books are abolished entirely and the common speech is used for writing, then the people in the capital who pull carts and sell soup speak a language that, on examination, has a grammar, unlike the twittering of people from Fujian and Guangdong, which has none. On this basis every petty pedlar in Beijing and Tianjin could be employed as a professor. As for the claim that *The Water Margin* and *The Dream of the Red Chamber* are the sages of the vernacular and fit to be textbooks: these people do not know that many turns of phrase in *The Water Margin* are taken from Yue Ke's *Jintuo cuibian*, and that *The Red Chamber* is not the work of one hand; their authors had read widely in all books. In short, unless one has read ten thousand volumes to tatters, one cannot write classical prose, nor can one write the vernacular. ……

I am a man of Fujian, a southern barbarian with a shrike's tongue, and I too wish to learn the language of the Central Plain. If those who teach me did not use the language of the Central Plain, and left me speaking my shrike-tongued Fujian dialect, would that do? In short, to preserve the national essence and teach the *Shuowen* is right; to make the *Shuowen* the guest and the vernacular the host is wrong. ……

Lin Shu kowtows.

## Notes

**The argument.** Lin makes three points against the vernacular:

| Point | Words |
|---|---|
| If the common speech is the standard, any street seller could teach. | 則凡京津之稗販，均可用為教授矣 |
| The great vernacular novels were written by men steeped in classical learning. | 作者均博極群書之人 |
| One cannot write good vernacular without classical learning. | 非讀破萬卷，不能為古文，亦並不能為白話 |

The third point concedes more than it seems: Lin accepts that the vernacular can be written well.
His objection is to making it the main thing (以白話為主).

**Reductio ad absurdum.** 據此，則…… "on this basis, then …" draws an absurd conclusion from the
opponent's view: if speech is the standard, pedlars are professors. The ironic 矣 marks it.

**A Fujianese on the national language.** Lin mocks his own dialect with Mencius' insult to a
southerner (南蠻鴃舌之人, *Mencius* 3A.4) and argues that a standard language is needed for people
like him. The irony is that the vernacular reformers agreed: they wanted a national spoken standard
(國語). The disagreement was whether writing should be based on it.

**A literary letter.** The form is a letter between equals: 足下 for Cai, 弟 for himself, 頓首 at
the end. 太史 honours Cai as a former member of the Hanlin Academy (he had passed the highest
examination in 1892). Lin writes to the chancellor of a modern university as one Qing scholar to
another. See [Letter formulas](../../grammar/epistolary-formulas.md).

**No vernacular at all.** Unlike Chen Duxiu's essay in [On literary revolution](u20-p01-on-literary-revolution.md),
Lin's letter contains no vernacular words. Even 白話 and 土語 are named, not used. Compare Cai
Yuanpei's reply in [Cai Yuanpei replies](u20-p03-cai-yuanpei-replies.md). See
[Mixed registers](../../grammar/mixed-register.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What does Lin mean by 則凡京津之稗販，均可用為教授矣?

- [ ] Pedlars in Beijing should be educated.
- [x] If the common speech were the standard, any street seller could be a professor, which is absurd.
- [ ] Professors should learn from pedlars.

### 2. short-answer

What does Lin concede about the vernacular, and what does he still reject?

**Answer:** He concedes that good vernacular can be written, but only by people steeped in classical learning. He rejects making the vernacular primary (以白話為主) and abolishing the old books.

### 3. short-answer

Which formulas show that the letter is written between equals?

**Answer:** 足下 for the recipient, 弟 for himself, and 頓首 at the end.

### 4. translate

Translate 非讀破萬卷，不能為古文，亦並不能為白話。

**Answer:** Unless one has read ten thousand volumes to tatters, one cannot write classical prose, nor can one write the vernacular.
