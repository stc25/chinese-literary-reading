## Introduction

In January 1917 the journal 《新青年》 "New Youth" printed Hu Shi's 〈文學改良芻議〉 "Modest
proposals for the reform of literature", which called for writing in the vernacular. The next
month its editor, Chen Duxiu, went further. His 〈文學革命論〉 "On literary revolution" declared
war on the whole literary tradition. Within a few years the vernacular (白話) had become the
written language of the new literature, of schools and eventually of the state.

Chen's essay is not itself in the vernacular. It is literary Chinese of the new periodical kind,
full of new terms, with one striking vernacular feature: the particle 的. Read the passage twice:
once for the argument, once to sort its words into literary, new and vernacular.

## Text

今日莊嚴燦爛之歐洲，何自而來乎？曰，革命之賜也。歐語所謂革命者，為革故更新之義，與中土所謂朝代鼎革，絕不相類；故自文藝復興以來，政治界有革命，宗教界亦有革命，倫理道德亦有革命，文學藝術，亦莫不有革命，莫不因革命而新興而進化。近代歐洲文明史，直可謂之「革命史」。故曰，今日莊嚴燦爛之歐洲，乃革命之賜也。

孔教問題，方喧呶於國中，此倫理道德革命之先聲也。文學革命之氣運，醞釀已非一日，其首舉義旗之急先鋒，則為吾友胡適。余甘冒全國學究之敵，高張「文學革命軍」大旗，以為吾友之聲援。旗上大書特書吾革命軍三大主義：曰「推倒雕琢的、阿諛的貴族文學，建設平易的、抒情的國民文學」；曰「推倒陳腐的、鋪張的古典文學，建設新鮮的、立誠的寫實文學」；曰「推倒迂晦的、艱澀的山林文學，建設明了的、通俗的社會文學」。

## Translation

Where did the majestic and splendid Europe of today come from? I answer: it is the gift of revolution. What European languages call revolution means doing away with the old and renewing; it is entirely different from what China calls the change of dynasty. So since the Renaissance there has been revolution in politics, revolution in religion, revolution in ethics and morality; and literature and art, too, have all had their revolutions, and all have risen anew and progressed because of revolution. The history of modern European civilisation may simply be called "the history of revolution". Therefore I say: the majestic and splendid Europe of today is the gift of revolution.

The question of Confucianism is just now being loudly debated in the country: this is the herald of a revolution in ethics and morality. The momentum of a literary revolution has been brewing for a long time, and the vanguard who first raised the banner of revolt is my friend Hu Shi. I am willing to brave the enmity of every pedant in the country and hold high the great banner of the "Army of Literary Revolution", in support of my friend. On the banner, in large characters, are the three great principles of our revolutionary army: overthrow the over-ornamented, flattering literature of the aristocracy, and build a plain, expressive literature of the people; overthrow the stale, pompous classical literature, and build a fresh, sincere realist literature; overthrow the obscure, difficult literature of recluses, and build a clear, popular literature of society.

## Notes

**Three layers of vocabulary.** The passage mixes three kinds of words:

| Layer | Examples |
|---|---|
| literary function words and patterns | 何自而來乎, 曰, 所謂……者, 莫不, 直可謂之, 乃……也, 余, 吾 |
| new terms (often from Japanese) | 革命 (new sense), 文藝復興, 宗教, 倫理, 道德, 主義, 貴族, 古典, 寫實, 社會, -界 |
| vernacular | 的 in the slogans |

The grammar is literary; the vocabulary is new; and in the slogans a vernacular particle appears.
See [Mixed registers](../../grammar/mixed-register.md).

**的 in the slogans.** In literary Chinese a modifier is joined to its noun with 之 or nothing.
Chen writes 雕琢的、阿諛的貴族文學 "over-ornamented, flattering aristocratic literature". 的 is the
vernacular equivalent of 之, and it also translates Japanese の and the English suffixes "-ic, -al,
-ous". Stacking adjectives with 的 (A的、B的 noun) became a mark of the new style. See
[Vernacular elements](../../grammar/vernacular-elements.md).

**革命: an old word redefined.** Chen begins by separating two senses. The old 革命 "changing the
mandate" meant one dynasty replacing another (the phrase comes from the *Book of Changes*: 湯武革命).
The new 革命 "revolution" is 革故更新 "doing away with the old and renewing". Compare
[New terms](../../grammar/neologisms.md).

**Rhetoric.** The opening is a question answered by the writer himself (……乎？曰……), and the first
paragraph ends by repeating its first sentence (故曰，今日莊嚴燦爛之歐洲，乃革命之賜也). The slogans
are three parallel sentences, each with the pattern 推倒 A的、B的 X文學，建設 C的、D的 Y文學. This
is the parallelism of the old style serving a new cause; see [Parallelism](../../grammar/parallelism.md).

**Hu Shi.** Hu Shi's own essay is not in this course: he died in 1962, so his works are not yet in
the public domain under the course's rule. It is worth reading: it argued for the vernacular in
literary Chinese.

## Exercises

### 1. choice

How does Chen distinguish the European idea of revolution from the Chinese 朝代鼎革?

- [ ] It means the change of dynasty.
- [x] It means doing away with the old and renewing, in every field.
- [ ] It means a return to antiquity.

### 2. short-answer

Which vernacular particle appears in the three slogans, and what literary word does it replace?

**Answer:** 的, which replaces 之 (or no particle) between a modifier and its noun.

### 3. short-answer

List the three kinds of literature Chen wants to overthrow and the three he wants to build.

**Answer:** Overthrow aristocratic (貴族), classical (古典) and recluse (山林) literature; build a literature of the people (國民), realist (寫實) and social (社會) literature.

### 4. translate

**Answer:** What European languages call revolution means doing away with the old and renewing; it is entirely different from what China calls the change of dynasty.
