## Introduction

On 11 June 1898 the Guangxu Emperor issued this edict "making clear the fundamental policy
of the state" (明定國是). It opened the Hundred Days' Reform (百日維新), a summer of reform
edicts that ended in September with the Empress Dowager's coup.

An edict (上諭) was issued through the Grand Secretariat (内閣) and copied out in the court
gazette. It has a fixed frame: the date, 内閣奉上諭 "the Grand Secretariat received the
following edict", the emperor's own words (朕 "We"), orders introduced by 著, and the
closing 欽此. This passage gives the opening argument and the closing order. It opens in the
**vertical, unpunctuated** view: find the frame first, then divide the rest.

## Text

光緖二十四年四月二十三日，内閣奉上諭：數年以來，中外臣工講求時務，多主變法自强。邇者詔書數下，如開特科，裁冘兵，改武科制度，立大小學堂，皆經再三審定，籌之至熟，甫議施行。惟是風氣尙未大開，論説莫衷一是，或託於老成憂國，以為舊章必應墨守，新法必當擯除，衆喙嘵嘵，空言無補。試問今日時局如此，國勢如此，若仍以不練之兵，有限之餉，士無實學，工無良師，强弱相形，貧富懸絶，豈真能制梃以撻堅甲利兵乎？……

京師大學堂為各行省之倡，尤應首先舉辦，著軍機大臣、總理各國事務王大臣會同妥速議奏。……將此通諭知之。欽此。

## Translation

On the 23rd day of the 4th month of the 24th year of Guangxu, the Grand Secretariat received the following edict: For several years now, officials at court and in the provinces have studied current affairs, and most have advocated reform and self-strengthening. Recently edicts have been issued one after another: setting up a special examination, cutting surplus troops, reforming the military examinations, establishing schools of all levels. All were examined again and again and planned most thoroughly, and it has only just been proposed to put them into effect. But attitudes are not yet open, and opinions cannot agree. Some take cover behind experience and concern for the country, holding that the old institutions must be held fast and the new methods rejected. Many mouths clamour, but empty words are of no help. Let us ask: with the situation as it is today, and the state's strength as it is, if we still rely on untrained troops and limited funds, with scholars lacking practical learning and artisans lacking good teachers, the strong and the weak set side by side, the rich and the poor so far apart, can we really make clubs to beat strong armour and sharp weapons? …

The Imperial University in the capital is to be an example to the provinces and above all should be set up first. Let the Grand Councillors and the princes and ministers of the Zongli Yamen deliberate on it jointly and report properly and quickly by memorial. … Let this be proclaimed to all. Respect this.

## Notes

**The frame of an edict.**

| Part | Here | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| date | 光緖二十四年四月二十三日 | |
| channel | 内閣奉上諭 | "the Grand Secretariat received the edict": the edict is quoted as received |
| body | 數年以來… | the emperor's words, in the first person (朕, not used in this excerpt) |
| orders | 著軍機大臣…議奏 | 著 "let …": a command to named officials |
| close | 將此通諭知之。欽此。 | "let this be proclaimed to all. Respect this." |

When an official later quoted the edict, it ended with 欽此, and he added 欽遵 "respectfully
complying". You will see this in [A seal and a rescript](u13-p03-a-seal-and-a-rescript.md).
See [Edict formulas](../../grammar/edict-formulas.md).

**Elevation.** In the original, 上諭 starts a new column and is raised one character above the
others (單抬). The Original text marks this with a space. See
[Elevation (抬頭) and spacing](../../grammar/elevation.md).

**中外.** In Qing official language 中外 usually means "at court and in the provinces", not
"in China and abroad": 中外臣工 are all officials, in the capital and outside it.

**An allusion from 孟子.** 制梃以撻堅甲利兵 comes from 孟子 (梁惠王上), where Mencius claims a
benevolent ruler's people could 制梃以撻秦楚之堅甲利兵 "make clubs and beat the strong armour and sharp
weapons of Qin and Chu". The edict turns it round: in the modern world, virtue alone will not
beat modern arms. 墨守 is an allusion too: 墨子 was famous for defending cities.

**Variant characters.** The transcription keeps the forms of the original: 緖, 强, 冘, 尙, 説, 衆, 絶.

**A long sentence.** 若仍以…豈真能…乎 is one sentence: "if we still rely on … (six phrases of
four characters) …, can we really …?" The run of four-character phrases builds up the
picture of weakness before the rhetorical question.

### 1. choice

What does 内閣奉上諭 tell you?

- [ ] The Grand Secretariat wrote the edict.
- [x] The Grand Secretariat received the emperor's edict and passed it on.
- [ ] The edict was sent to the Grand Secretariat for discussion.

### 2. choice

What does 著 mean in 著軍機大臣…會同妥速議奏?

- [ ] to write
- [x] an order: "let … (do)"
- [ ] wearing

### 3. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 惟是風氣尙未大開論説莫衷一是

**Answer:** 惟是風氣尙未大開，論説莫衷一是，

### 4. short-answer

What do 中外臣工 mean in Qing official language?

**Answer:** Officials at court and in the provinces (all officials), not "Chinese and foreign officials".

### 5. short-answer

Mencius said that a benevolent ruler's people could 制梃以撻秦楚之堅甲利兵. How does the edict use the allusion?

**Answer:** It turns it into a rhetorical question: with untrained troops and no practical learning, China cannot really beat modern arms with clubs. Virtue without reform is not enough.
