## Introduction

For thirteen centuries the civil service examinations (科舉) had chosen China's officials. In
August 1905 six of the most powerful provincial governors, led by Yuan Shikai and Zhang Zhidong,
sent a joint memorial asking for them to be abolished at once in favour of modern schools.
Four days later an edict granted the request. The examinations were never held again.

A memorial (奏摺) is a report or request from an official to the emperor. It too has a fixed
form: a one-sentence summary (奏為…事), the body opening with 竊 "humbly", and a closing
request (擬請, 伏乞). This passage gives the memorial's summary, its opening and its
conclusion, and then the beginning of the edict that answers it.

## Text

奏為時艱日迫，亟圖補救，擬請立停科舉推廣學校，並妥籌辦法以期有利無弊，恭折會陳，仰祈聖鑑事。

竊維科舉之弊，古今人言之綦詳，而科舉之阻礙學堂，妨害人才，臣世凱、臣之洞等亦迭經陳奏，久在聖明昭鑑之中，無須縷述，以瀆宸聽。……

故欲補救時艱，必自推廣學校始，而欲推廣學校，必自先停科舉始。擬請宸衷獨斷，雷厲風行，立沛綸音，停罷科舉，庶幾廣學育才，化民成俗，內定國是，外服強鄰，轉危為安，胥基於此。

八月初四日內閣奉上諭：袁世凱等奏請立停科舉以廣學校，並妥籌辦法一摺。……茲據該督等奏稱，科舉不停，民間相率觀望，欲推廣學堂，必先停科舉等語，所陳不為無見。著即自丙午科為始，所有鄉會試一律停止，各省嵗科考試亦即停止。……欽此。

## Translation

Memorial concerning the following: the times are hard and grow more pressing every day, and a remedy must urgently be sought. We propose to request that the examinations be stopped at once and schools expanded, and that arrangements be carefully planned so that there are advantages without drawbacks. We report jointly in this respectful memorial, and look up and pray for Your Majesty's inspection.

We humbly consider that the defects of the examinations have been fully described by people past and present, and that your servants Shikai, Zhidong and others have repeatedly memorialised on how the examinations obstruct the schools and harm talent. This has long been in Your Majesty's clear view, and there is no need to set it out in detail and so trouble the imperial hearing. …

So if the hardships of the times are to be remedied, it must begin with expanding the schools; and if the schools are to be expanded, it must begin with first stopping the examinations. We propose to request that Your Majesty decide alone, swiftly and vigorously, issue the imperial word at once, and abolish the examinations, so that learning may be spread and talent nurtured, the people transformed and good customs formed; that at home the course of the state may be settled and abroad strong neighbours won over; that danger may be turned into safety. All rests on this.

On the 4th day of the 8th month the Grand Secretariat received the following edict: Yuan Shikai and others have memorialised requesting that the examinations be stopped at once and schools expanded, and that arrangements be carefully planned. … Now the said governors-general state that while the examinations are not stopped, the people all wait and see, and that to expand the schools the examinations must first be stopped. What they set out is not without insight. Let it be ordered that from the bingwu examination onwards all provincial and metropolitan examinations be stopped without exception, and the annual and qualifying examinations in the provinces also be stopped. … Respect this.

## Notes

**The frame of a memorial.**

| Part | Here | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| summary | 奏為…事 | "memorial concerning …": the whole request in one sentence, used as its title |
| opening | 竊維… | "we humbly consider": the body begins |
| request | 擬請… | "we propose to request" |
| close (not in this excerpt) | 伏乞皇上聖鑒。謹奏。 | "prostrate, we beg Your Majesty's inspection. Respectfully memorialised." |

See [Memorial formulas](../../grammar/memorial-formulas.md).

**Humble and honorific language.** The officials call themselves 臣 "your servant" followed by
their given name only (臣世凱, 臣之洞). Everything of the emperor's is exalted: 聖鑑 his
inspection, 聖明 his wisdom, 宸聽 his hearing, 宸衷 his heart, 綸音 his word. Their own acts
are humble: 竊 "humbly, privately", 仰祈 "look up and pray", 瀆 "trouble, defile". In the
original, the honorific words would have been elevated. See
[Honorific and humble forms](../../grammar/honorific-humble.md).

**Parallel argument.** 欲補救時艱，必自推廣學校始；欲推廣學校，必自先停科舉始 is a chain:
the end of the first clause becomes the start of the second (a figure called 頂針 dǐngzhēn).
The request then ends with three pairs of four characters: 廣學育才，化民成俗 / 內定國是，外服強鄰 / 轉危為安.

**How the edict cites the memorial.** The edict names the memorial (袁世凱等奏請…一摺),
summarises its argument (茲據該督等奏稱 … 等語), judges it (所陳不為無見), and decides (著即…).
This pattern of citing a document before acting on it runs through all official writing. See
[Citing earlier documents](../../grammar/embedded-citation.md).

**不為無見.** A double negative, "not without insight": a cautious approval, typical of an emperor's voice.

**丙午科.** The next examinations were due in the bingwu year (1906). Years in the cycle of
sixty (干支) name examinations, wars and reforms. See [Dates and calendars](../../toolkit/t02-dates-and-calendars.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What is the function of 奏為…事?

- [ ] It closes the memorial.
- [x] It summarises the whole memorial in one sentence.
- [ ] It quotes an earlier edict.

### 2. choice

Why do the officials write 臣世凱, 臣之洞 with their given names only?

- [ ] Their surnames were taboo.
- [x] Before the emperor, an official calls himself 臣 "your servant" with his given name: a humble form.
- [ ] To save space.

### 3. short-answer

List three words in the memorial that exalt the emperor.

**Answer:** Any three of 聖鑑, 聖明, 昭鑑, 宸聽, 宸衷, 綸音.

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 故欲補救時艱必自推廣學校始而欲推廣學校必自先停科舉始

**Answer:** 故欲補救時艱，必自推廣學校始，而欲推廣學校，必自先停科舉始。

### 5. short-answer

How does the edict introduce and close its summary of the memorial?

**Answer:** It introduces it with 茲據該督等奏稱 "now the said governors-general state" and closes it with 等語 "… and so on".
