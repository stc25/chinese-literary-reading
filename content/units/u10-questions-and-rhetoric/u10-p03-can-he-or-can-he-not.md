## Introduction

Four rhetorical questions from the middle of 〈少年中國說〉. Liang has just described a
series of figures who have outlived their glory, from an ageing courtesan to Napoleon in
exile: 美人豪傑 "beautiful women and heroes" refers to them. He then turns from people to China
itself. Each paragraph here is a separate sentence; the text between them is left out.

## Text

美人豪傑且然，而況於尋常碌碌者耶？

於此人也，而欲望以拏雲之手段，回天之事功，挾山超海之意氣，能乎不能？

歷史家所鋪叙，詞章家所謳歌，何一非我國民少年時代良辰美景、賞心樂事之陳跡哉！

十八省之土地財產，已爲人懷中之肉；四百兆之父兄子弟，已爲人注籍之奴，豈所謂「老大嫁作商人婦」者耶？

## Translation

If even beautiful women and heroes are like this, how much more so are ordinary, mediocre people!

As for such a person, to expect him to have the skill to grasp the clouds, the achievements that turn heaven around, the spirit that carries mountains and leaps over seas: can he or can he not?

What historians narrate and poets sing: which of it is not a trace of the fine days and beautiful scenes, the delights and joys of our nation's youth?

The land and property of the eighteen provinces have already become meat in other people's arms; the four hundred million fathers, brothers and sons have already become slaves on other people's registers. Is this not what is called "grown old, she married and became a merchant's wife"?

## Notes

**Questions that make statements.** None of these questions expects an answer. Each is a
forceful way of stating something:

| Pattern | Example | Means |
|---|---|---|
| A 且然，而況 B 耶？ | 美人豪傑且然，而況於尋常碌碌者耶？ | if even A is like this, B certainly is |
| V 乎不 V？ | 能乎不能？ | can he? (no, he cannot) |
| 何一非 …哉！ | 何一非…之陳跡哉！ | every one of them is… |
| 豈 … 耶？ | 豈所謂…者耶？ | is it not…? (yes, it is) |

See [Rhetorical questions](../../grammar/rhetorical-questions.md).

**The punctuation.** The edition ends 何一非…哉 with ！ rather than ？: the editors read it as an
exclamation. Questions and exclamations often overlap in literary Chinese, and 哉 can serve
both. Modern punctuation is an interpretation.

**The quotation.** 老大嫁作商人婦 comes from Bai Juyi's 白居易 poem 〈琵琶行〉 (816), about a
once-famous courtesan who, grown old, married a merchant. Liang compares China, whose land and
people now belong to others, to her. See [Allusion and quotation](../../grammar/allusion.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What does 美人豪傑且然，**而況**於尋常碌碌者耶 mean?

- [ ] Beautiful women and heroes are like this, but ordinary people are not.
- [x] If even beautiful women and heroes are like this, ordinary people certainly are.
- [ ] Are beautiful women and heroes like ordinary people?

### 2. choice

What does **何一非**…之陳跡哉 say about the things historians and poets record?

- [ ] Only one of them is a trace of China's youth.
- [x] Every one of them is a trace of China's youth.
- [ ] None of them is a trace of China's youth.

**Explanation:** "Which one is not…?" expects the answer "none": all of them are.

### 3. choice

What answer does 豈所謂「老大嫁作商人婦」者耶 expect?

- [x] Yes: this is exactly what is meant.
- [ ] No: this is not what is meant.
- [ ] No answer: it is a real question.

### 4. punctuate

Punctuate the sentence.

**Text:** 美人豪傑且然而況於尋常碌碌者耶

**Answer:** 美人豪傑且然，而況於尋常碌碌者耶？

### 5. short-answer

To what does Liang compare China in the last sentence, and why?

**Answer:** To the ageing courtesan in Bai Juyi's poem, who married a merchant: China's land and people have come to belong to others.
