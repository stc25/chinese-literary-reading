## Introduction

On 12 February 1912 the last Qing emperor abdicated. Yuan Shikai, the Qing prime minister
who had negotiated the abdication, at once telegraphed the revolutionary government in
Nanjing. Sun Yat-sen replied by offering to resign in Yuan's favour, as he had promised. The
gazette printed both telegrams.

Telegrams (電報, 電) were the fastest way to communicate, and they had their own conventions:
a list of addressees ending 鑒 "for your inspection", a terse style, the sender's given name,
and a date written as a **single character** (the 韻目代日 system). This passage gives
Yuan's telegram in full and the beginning and end of Sun's reply.

## Text

萬急。南京孫大總統、參議院各部總長、武昌黎副總統同鑒：共和為最良國體，世界之公認，今由弊政一躍而躋及之，實諸公累年之心血，亦民國無窮之幸福。大清皇帝旣明詔辭位，業經世凱署名，則宣布之日，為帝政之終局，卽民國之始基。從此努力進行，務令達到圓滿地位，永不使君主政體再行於中國。現在統一組織至重且繁，世凱極願南行，暢聆大敎，共謀進行之法，祗因北方秩序不易維持，軍旅如林，須加部署，而東北人心未盡一致，稍有動搖，牽涉各國，諸君洞察時局，必能諒此苦衷。至共和建設重要問題，諸君研究有素，成算在胸，應如何協商組織統一治法，尙希迅卽見敎。世凱。眞。

萬急。北京袁慰亭先生鑒：電悉。文以菲才，辱膺國民推戴，受任以來，拮据張皇，力不副願，嘗恐覆餗貽羞，負國民委託之重。自慚受任無狀，日夜希冀推賢讓能，苟得如公者舉而自代，……新舊交替，萬機待舉，遣大投艱，非公莫辦，謹虛左位，以俟明哲，曷勝佇立翹望之至。孫文。元。

## Translation

Most urgent. For the inspection of President Sun, the Senate and the ministers in Nanjing, and Vice-President Li in Wuchang. A republic is the best form of state, as the world generally recognises. That we have now reached it in one leap from bad government is the fruit of your years of labour, gentlemen, and the endless good fortune of the Republic. Now that the Emperor of the Great Qing has abdicated by public edict, with my countersignature, the day of its proclamation is the end of imperial rule and the foundation of the Republic. From now on we must work hard to go forward and make sure of reaching a satisfactory state, never letting monarchy be practised in China again. At present the organisation of a unified government is most important and complex. I very much wish to come south to hear your teachings in full and plan together how to proceed, but order in the north is not easy to keep: the troops are as many as trees in a forest and must be deployed, and in the north-east popular feeling is not yet entirely united, so that the slightest disturbance would involve the foreign powers. You gentlemen see the situation clearly and will surely understand my difficult position. As for the important questions of building the republic, you have long studied them and have plans ready; I further hope you will instruct me at once how to consult on organising a unified government. Shikai. The 11th.

Most urgent. For the inspection of Mr Yuan Weiting in Beijing. Your telegram is noted. With my meagre talent I was unworthily honoured with the people's election. Since taking office I have struggled in bewilderment, my strength not matching my wishes, and I have always feared that I would fail in office, bring shame on myself, and betray the weight of the people's trust. Ashamed that I have achieved nothing in office, I have hoped day and night to give way to the worthy and able: if I could find someone like you and put him forward to replace me … In this change from old to new, a thousand affairs await action; for these great and difficult tasks, no one but you can manage. I respectfully leave the seat of honour empty to await the wise one, and cannot express how eagerly I stand waiting. Sun Wen. The 13th.

## Notes

**The form of a telegram.**

| Part | Yuan | Sun |
|---|---|---|
| priority | 萬急 | 萬急 |
| addressees, with 鑒 | 南京孫大總統…同鑒 | 北京袁慰亭先生鑒 |
| acknowledgement | | 電悉 "your telegram is noted" |
| body | 共和為最良國體… | 文以菲才… |
| sender (given name only) | 世凱 | 孫文 |
| date code | 眞 | 元 |

**Dates in one character (韻目代日).** To save money, telegrams gave the day of the month as a
single character, taken from the rhyme categories (韻目) of the traditional rhyme books: 東 = 1,
冬 = 2, 江 = 3 … 眞 = 11, 文 = 12, 元 = 13 … 卅 = 30, 世 or 引 = 31. The month is not given:
you work it out from context. Historians still name events this way: the 馬日事變 "the 馬-day
incident" (21 May 1927). See [Dates and calendars](../../toolkit/t02-dates-and-calendars.md).

**Humility and courtesy.** Both men write of themselves by given name (世凱, 文) and humbly
(菲才, 辱膺); both address the other with respect (諸公, 諸君, 公, 先生, 大敎, 見敎). Sun uses
Yuan's courtesy name 慰亭, a mark of respect between equals. Notice that Yuan addresses Sun
as 大總統 "President" while he himself does not yet hold an office in the Republic.

**Allusions.** 覆餗 comes from the 易經 (鼎卦): a cauldron with a broken leg that spills the
duke's stew, an image of an official unequal to his post. 虛左 "leaving the left seat
empty" is from the 史記: the Lord of Xinling left the seat of honour empty for a worthy man.
遣大投艱 echoes the 尚書 (大誥). With these, Sun's offer to resign becomes the classic gesture of
yielding to a worthier man.

**Political meaning.** Behind the courtesy, both telegrams are political moves. Yuan declines
to come south to Nanjing (祗因北方秩序不易維持 …), where the revolutionaries wanted him to take
office; Sun offers him the presidency. The question of the capital, Nanjing or Beijing,
divided them for the next month.

**電悉.** "Your telegram is noted" is the telegraphic counterpart of 呈悉 and 稟悉, which open
rulings. See [Republican document types](../../grammar/document-types.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What does 眞 at the end of Yuan's telegram stand for?

- [ ] "sincerely"
- [x] the date: the 11th day of the month
- [ ] Yuan's courtesy name

### 2. short-answer

Why does Yuan say he cannot come south?

**Answer:** Order in the north is hard to keep: the troops must be deployed, and popular feeling in the north-east is not yet united, so any disturbance could involve the foreign powers.

### 3. choice

What does Sun mean by 謹虛左位，以俟明哲?

- [ ] He is moving his office to the left wing.
- [x] He is leaving the seat of honour (the presidency) empty for a wiser man: Yuan.
- [ ] He is waiting for the Senate to decide.

### 4. short-answer

List two ways in which Sun is humble about himself and two in which he honours Yuan.

**Answer:** Humble: 菲才, 辱膺, 受任無狀, writing his own given name 文. Honouring: the courtesy name 袁慰亭, 先生, 公, 如公者, 非公莫辦.
