## Introduction

Wang Guowei, whose essay on new terms you read in
[Words from Japan](../u16-the-press-and-new-vocabulary/u16-p02-words-from-japan.md), turned in
the 1910s to the newly found oracle bones of the Shang. In 〈殷周制度論〉 "On the institutions of
the Shang and Zhou" (1917) he used them, with the classics, to argue that the change from Shang
to Zhou was the greatest break in early Chinese history: the Zhou invented the rule that the
throne passes to the eldest son of the principal wife.

The essay is a model of the new scholarship: a bold thesis, set out at the start, and then
evidence from sources quoted and weighed one against another. Watch how Wang announces his
thesis, lists his points (一曰……二曰……三曰……), introduces his sources (……之說曰, 所謂……者)
and judges them.

## Text

中國政治與文化之變革，莫劇於殷、周之際。……殷、周間之大變革，自其表言之，不過一姓一家之興亡與都邑之移轉；自其裏言之，則舊制度廢而新制度興，舊文化廢而新文化興。又自其表言之，則古聖人之所以取天下及所以守之者，若無以異於後世之帝王；而自其裏言之，則其制度、文物與其立制之本意，乃出於萬世治安之大計，其心術與規摹，迥非後世帝王所能夢見也。

欲觀周之所以定天下，必自其制度始矣。周人制度之大異於商者，一曰立子立嫡之制，由是而生宗法及喪服之制，並由是而有封建子弟之制、君天子臣諸侯之制；二曰廟數之制；三曰同姓不婚之制。此數者，皆周之所以綱紀天下。其旨則在納上下於道德，而合天子、諸侯、卿、大夫、士、庶民以成一道德之團體。周公制作之本意，實在於此。此非穿鑿附會之言也，茲篇所論，皆有事實爲之根據，試略述之。

……其條例則《春秋左氏傳》之說曰：「太子死，有母弟則立之，無則立長。年鈞擇賢，義鈞則卜。」公羊家之說曰：「禮，嫡夫人無子立右媵，右媵無子立左媵，……」此二說中，後說尤爲詳密，顧皆後儒充類之說，當立法之初，未必窮其變至此。然所謂「立子以貴不以長，立適以長不以賢」者，乃傳子法之精髓，當時雖未必有此語，固已用此意矣。

## Translation

Of all the changes in China's government and culture, none is more drastic than that at the transition from Shang to Zhou. …… The great change between Shang and Zhou, seen from the surface, was no more than the rise and fall of one family and the moving of the capital; seen from within, old institutions fell and new institutions arose, old culture fell and new culture arose. Again, seen from the surface, the ways in which the ancient sages won the empire and kept it seem no different from those of later emperors; but seen from within, their institutions, their culture and their original intention in founding the institutions came in fact from a grand plan for order and peace for ten thousand generations, and their thinking and design were far beyond anything later emperors could dream of.

If one wishes to see how the Zhou settled the empire, one must begin from its institutions. The Zhou institutions that differ greatly from the Shang are, first, the institution of succession by the son and by the son of the principal wife, from which arose the lineage system and the mourning system, and from which came the enfeoffment of sons and younger brothers and the system in which the Son of Heaven is lord and the feudal lords are subjects; second, the number of ancestral temples; third, the rule that those of the same surname do not marry. These are all the means by which the Zhou gave order to the empire. Their purpose was to bring high and low within morality, and to unite the Son of Heaven, the feudal lords, ministers, great officers, gentlemen and the common people into one moral community. The Duke of Zhou's original intention in making institutions truly lay here. These are not far-fetched or forced words: everything this essay argues has facts as its evidence. Let me set it out briefly.

…… The rules are given in the account of the *Zuo Commentary*: "When the heir dies, if there is a younger brother by the same mother, he is made heir; if not, the eldest is made heir. If they are equal in age, the worthiest is chosen; if equal in merit, the matter is divined." The Gongyang school's account says: "By the rites, if the principal wife has no son, the son of the right secondary wife is made heir; if the right secondary wife has no son, the son of the left secondary wife is made heir; ……" Of these two accounts, the latter is especially detailed, but both are the extrapolations of later Confucians; when the rule was first laid down, its makers did not necessarily work out every case in this way. Yet what is called "a son is made heir by rank, not by age; the son of the principal wife is made heir by age, not by worth" is the essence of the rule of succession by sons; though these words may not have been said at the time, the idea was certainly already in use.

## Notes

**Thesis first.** Wang states his claim in the first sentence (莫劇於殷、周之際) and the plan of the
essay in the second paragraph: three institutions, numbered 一曰、二曰、三曰. Then he promises
evidence: 茲篇所論，皆有事實爲之根據 "everything this essay argues has facts as its evidence".
The words 事實 "facts" and 根據 "evidence" are new terms, and they mark the new scholarship. See
[Structuring an argument](../../grammar/argument-structure.md).

**Surface and depth.** 自其表言之……自其裏言之…… "seen from the surface … seen from within"
is a parallel frame used twice. The surface is the dynastic story that traditional histories tell;
the depth is institutions and culture, which Wang wants to study.

**Weighing sources.** Wang quotes two accounts of the rules of succession, one from the *Zuo
Commentary* and one from the Gongyang school, and then judges them:

| Step | Words |
|---|---|
| introduce the source | 《春秋左氏傳》之說曰：「……」; 公羊家之說曰：「……」 |
| compare | 此二說中，後說尤爲詳密 |
| criticise | 顧皆後儒充類之說 "but both are later extrapolations" |
| keep what is valid | 然所謂「……」者，乃傳子法之精髓 |

He treats the classics as historical sources of different dates, not as authorities. See
[Commentary and citation](../../grammar/citation-conventions.md).

**之說曰 and 所謂.** ……之說曰 "the account of … says" introduces a quotation from a work or a school.
所謂「……」者 "what is called '…'" picks up a well-known phrase (here from the Gongyang commentary on
the first year of Duke Yin) and makes it the subject of a new sentence.

**君天子臣諸侯.** 君 "lord" and 臣 "subject" are nouns used as verbs: "to treat the Son of Heaven as
lord and the feudal lords as subjects". See [Word classes](../../grammar/word-classes.md).

## Exercises

### 1. choice

According to Wang, what was the greatest change at the transition from Shang to Zhou, seen from within?

- [ ] the moving of the capital
- [ ] the rise of a new ruling family
- [x] the fall of old institutions and culture and the rise of new ones

### 2. short-answer

Name the three Zhou institutions that Wang says differ from the Shang.

**Answer:** Succession by the son and by the son of the principal wife (立子立嫡之制); the number of ancestral temples (廟數之制); no marriage between those of the same surname (同姓不婚之制).

### 3. short-answer

What is Wang's judgement of the Zuo and Gongyang accounts of succession?

**Answer:** The Gongyang account is more detailed, but both are extrapolations by later Confucians; the original rule did not work out every case. Still, the principle 立子以貴不以長，立適以長不以賢 was in use.
