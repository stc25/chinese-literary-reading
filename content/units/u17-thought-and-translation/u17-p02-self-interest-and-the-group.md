## Introduction

How can creatures that struggle for survival live together in societies? Huxley's answer,
in the thirteenth chapter of his introduction, is sympathy: humans feel what others feel, and
from this grows conscience, which restrains self-interest. Yan Fu titles the chapter 制私
"Restraining the self".

Yan was not only a translator. At the end of many chapters he adds his own comments, headed
復案 "Fu's comment", and here he argues back: Huxley, he says, has cause and effect the wrong
way round. Read the passage as a debate. Find each side's thesis, and the words that mark
the steps of Yan's refutation: 然, 蓋, 夫, 然則, 此其…所以….

## Text

自營甚者必侈於自由，自由侈則侵，侵則爭，爭則群渙，群渙則人道所恃以為存者去。故曰自營大行，群道息而人種滅也。然而天地之性，物之最能為群者，又莫人若。如是則其所受於天，必有以制此自營者，夫而後有群之效也。……

獨愛子之情，人為獨摯，其種最貴，故其生有待於父母之保持，方諸物為最久。久，故其用愛也尤深。繼乃推類擴充，緣所愛而及所不愛。是故慈幼者仁之本也。而慈幼之事，又若從自營之私而起。由私生慈，由慈生仁，由仁勝私，此道之所以不測也。……

人心常德，皆本之能相感通而後有。於是是心之中，常有物焉以為之宰，字曰天良。天良者，保群之主，所以制自營之私，不使過用以敗群者也。

復案：赫胥黎保群之論，可謂辨矣。然其謂群道由人心善相感而立，則有倒果為因之病，又不可不知也。蓋人之由散入群，原為安利，其始正與禽獸下生等耳，初非由感通而立也。夫既以群為安利，則天演之事，將使能群者存，不群者滅；善群者存，不善群者滅。善群者何？善相感通者是。然則善相感通之德，乃天擇以後之事，非其始之即如是也。其始豈無不善相感通者？經物競之烈，亡矣，不可見矣。赫胥黎執其末以齊其本，此其言群理，所以不若斯賓塞氏之密也。且以感通為人道之本，其說發於計學家亞丹斯密，亦非赫胥黎氏所獨標之新理也。

## Translation

When self-interest is excessive, people go too far in doing as they like; when they go too far, they encroach on others; when they encroach, they fight; when they fight, the group falls apart; and when the group falls apart, that on which the human way relies to survive is lost. Hence it is said: when self-interest prevails, the way of the group ceases and the human species perishes. And yet, in the nature of all creation, of all creatures the best able to form groups, none is like man. This being so, what he has received from heaven must include the means to restrain this self-interest; only then can there be the benefits of the group. ……

Of love for one's offspring, man's is uniquely intense. His species is the most precious, and so his life depends on the care of his parents longer than that of any other creature. Because it is long, his love goes especially deep. Then he extends it by analogy, and from what he loves reaches out to what he does not love. Therefore loving the young is the root of benevolence. And yet the love of the young seems to arise from the selfishness of self-interest. From the self comes love; from love comes benevolence; and by benevolence the self is overcome. This is why the Way is unfathomable. ……

The constant virtues of the human heart are all rooted in the capacity to feel with one another. Thereupon there is always something within this heart that governs it, named conscience (天良). Conscience is the master that preserves the group: it restrains the selfishness of self-interest and prevents it from being used to excess and ruining the group.

Fu's comment: Huxley's argument on preserving the group may be called well argued. But when he says that the way of the group was established by the human heart's being good at feeling with others, he commits the fault of taking the effect for the cause, and this must be recognised. For people passed from scattered individuals into groups originally for safety and advantage; at the beginning they were no different from birds, beasts and lower forms of life, and groups were not at first established through sympathy. Now, since groups exist for safety and advantage, the process of evolution will make those able to form groups survive and those unable perish; those good at forming groups survive and those not good at it perish. Who are those good at forming groups? Those good at feeling with one another. That being so, the virtue of feeling with one another is something that came after the selection of heaven; it was not so from the beginning. At the beginning, were there surely no people who were bad at feeling with others? Through the fierceness of the struggle of things they perished and can no longer be seen. Huxley takes the branch and equates it with the root: this is why his account of the principles of society is not as rigorous as Mr Spencer's. Moreover, making sympathy the root of the human way is an idea that comes from the economist Adam Smith; it is not a new principle that Huxley alone put forward.

## Notes

**The shape of the debate.**

| Step | Words | Content |
|---|---|---|
| concession | 赫胥黎保群之論，可謂辨矣 | Huxley argues well. |
| objection | 然……則有倒果為因之病 | But he takes the effect for the cause. |
| reason | 蓋…… | Groups began for safety and advantage, not sympathy. |
| argument | 夫既……則…… | Evolution then selects those who form groups well. |
| conclusion | 然則…… | Sympathy is a result of selection, not its origin. |
| objection answered | 其始豈無……？……亡矣 | The unsympathetic existed, but died out. |
| verdict | 此其……所以…… | So Huxley is less rigorous than Spencer. |
| further point | 且…… | And the idea is not even his own. |

This is the classical way to refute: grant something first (可謂……矣), then turn with 然. See
[Structuring an argument](../../grammar/argument-structure.md).

**A chain of consequences.** The first sentence is a chain of 則 clauses, each beginning with the
last word of the one before (自由侈則侵，侵則爭，爭則群渙……). This figure, 頂真 dǐngzhēn, is
common in early Chinese argument, and it makes a long causal sequence easy to follow.
由私生慈，由慈生仁，由仁勝私 works the same way.

**群 and 私.** 群 "the group" is Yan Fu's word for society. He took it from 荀子 Xunzi, who says that
humans are stronger than oxen and horses because 人能群 "humans can form groups"; Yan called
sociology 群學. Against it stands 私 "the self, the private", and the old Confucian ideal that
the public (公) should overcome the private. Huxley's "self-assertion" becomes 自營, and his
"conscience" 天良, a term from Mencius' 良知良能 "innate knowledge and ability". In each case a
Western idea is given a Chinese philosophical pedigree.

**Domesticating an example.** Later in this chapter Huxley illustrates wounded pride with the
Biblical story of Haman, who hanged Mordecai for refusing to bow to him. Yan replaces it with a
Chinese story: the general Li Guang 李廣, who had a commandant of 霸陵 killed for insulting him.
In a note he gives Huxley's example and explains: 今以與李廣霸陵尉事相類，故易之如此 "since it
resembles the story of Li Guang and the commandant of Baling, I have changed it in this way".
This is Yan's 達 "fluency" at the cost of 信 "faithfulness".

**The translator as critic.** Yan's comment prefers Spencer, who held that societies, like
organisms, evolve through competition, to Huxley, who held that ethics must resist it. For readers
after 1898 the lesson was Spencer's: China had to become a group that could survive.

## Exercises

### 1. choice

What fault does Yan Fu find in Huxley's argument?

- [ ] He relies too much on Spencer.
- [x] He takes the effect for the cause.
- [ ] He ignores the struggle of things.

### 2. short-answer

According to Yan Fu, why did people first form groups, and how did the capacity to feel with others become general?

**Answer:** They formed groups for safety and advantage (安利). Evolution then made those good at forming groups, that is, those good at feeling with others, survive, and the others died out in the struggle.

### 3. short-answer

Which word does Yan use to turn from his concession to his objection, and which to draw his conclusion?

**Answer:** 然 "but" turns to the objection; 然則 "that being so" draws the conclusion.

### 4. translate

Translate 由私生慈，由慈生仁，由仁勝私。

**Answer:** From the self comes love; from love comes benevolence; and by benevolence the self is overcome.
