## Summary

Words for "I", "we" and "you", and how their use depends on register and politeness.

## Explanation

**No change of form.** The same word serves as "I", "me", "my" (and often "we", "us",
"our"): 吾見… "I saw…", 吾家 "our family". Position tells you which.

**"I", "we".**

| Word | Notes |
|---|---|
| 吾 wú | very common in essays and letters: 吾心目中 "in my mind's eye", 吾家 "our family" |
| 我 wǒ | common, often for "we/our" as a group: 我中國 "our China" |
| 余 yú, 予 yú | "I", common in letters, diaries and narrative: 余曰 "I said" |

**"You".** 爾 ěr and 汝 rǔ are used **to someone younger or of lower standing**: a father
to his son, an elder brother to a younger one, an uncle to a nephew. Zeng Guofan uses 爾 to
his sons and brothers; his uncle uses 汝 to him.

**Avoiding "you".** To an equal or a superior, writers avoid these words. They use a
kinship term, a title or a polite word instead: Zeng writes 弟 "younger brother" for "you"
(冀弟毋動肝氣), and his uncle is addressed as 舅 "uncle" (舅勿來). Letter writers also refer to themselves
humbly; see [Honorific and humble forms](honorific-humble.md).

**"He, she, it, they".** Literary Chinese has no common word for "he/she" as a subject.
The person is simply not mentioned, or named again. As an object, 之 "him, her, it,
them" is used (see [之 as object pronoun](zhi-object.md)); before a noun, 其 "his, her,
its, their" (see [其](qi-pronoun.md)).

## Examples

| Chinese | Pinyin | Translation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 吾見其寄岱雲信 | wú jiàn qí jì Dàiyún xìn | I have seen the letter he sent to Daiyun | u04-p03 |
| 吾家宜力矯此弊 | wú jiā yí lì jiǎo cǐ bì | our family must do its utmost to correct this fault | u04-p02 |
| 爾須與之互相勸勉 | ěr xū yǔ zhī hùxiāng quànmiǎn | you must encourage each other | u04-p01 |
| 余曰：「京城苦，舅勿來。」 | yú yuē: jīngchéng kǔ, jiù wù lái. | I said: "Life in the capital is hard. Uncle, do not come." | u03-p03 |
| 然吾終尋汝任所也 | rán wú zhōng xún rǔ rènsuǒ yě | but in the end I shall seek you out at your post | u03-p03 |
| 我中國其果老大矣乎 | wǒ Zhōngguó qí guǒ lǎodà yǐ hū | has our China really grown old? | u05-p01 |
