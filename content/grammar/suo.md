## Summary

所 before a verb makes a noun phrase for the object of that verb: "what is …", "that which …".

## Explanation

所 + verb means "**what** (someone) V-s", "**the thing that is** V-ed":

- 所用 "what is used"; 所得 "what is obtained"; 所知 "what (one) knows".

The phrase works as a noun. It can stand alone or modify another noun, usually with 之:

- 供犯罪所用之物 "things used for committing a crime";
- 因犯罪所得之物 "things obtained through crime".

**Who does the action?** The doer, if named, comes **before** 所: 吾所謂… "what I call…",
他人所有物 "things that other people own". So: doer + 所 + verb (+ 之 + noun).

**所 and 者.** They are mirror images:

| Phrase | Points to | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 所 + verb | the object, the thing done | 所得 "what is obtained" |
| verb + 者 | the doer | 殺人者 "the one who kills" |
| 所 + verb + 者 | the object, stressed | 有所知者 "whatever (you) know" |

**Fixed words with 所.** 所謂 "what is called, so-called"; 所屬 "what belongs (to one)",
"subjects"; 所以 "that by which" (see [所以](suoyi.md)). 所 also forms the passive with 為: see
[Passive constructions](passives.md).
