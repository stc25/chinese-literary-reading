## Summary

The pattern A者，B也 says what A is: "A is B". 者 marks the term being defined and 也 closes the definition.

## Explanation

The most common way to define something is:

> **A 者，B 也。** "A is B."

- **者** follows the term to be defined. It works like a spoken pause, or like
  bold type in a dictionary: "*A*: …".
- **也** closes the definition, as in any nominal sentence (see
  [也 ending a nominal sentence](ye-final.md)).

Example: 衝突者，調和之先驅也 "conflict is the forerunner of reconciliation".

**Variants.**

- **也者**: A 也者，B 也, a stronger form: 朝也者，一家之私產也 "a dynasty is the
  private property of one family".
- **云者**: A 云者 "what is called A", often used when the writer is explaining his own
  term: 新民云者… "what I call 'renewing the people'…".

**Do not confuse** 者 here with 者 after a verb meaning "the one who, those that"
(動者 "those that move"). The difference: in a definition, 者 follows the term being
defined and is followed by a pause; the comment ending in 也 says what the term is.
See [者: "the one who, that which"](zhe-nominaliser.md).
