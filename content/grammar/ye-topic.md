## Summary

也 after the opening phrase of a sentence marks a pause: the phrase is the topic, and what follows is the comment on it ("as for X, …").

## Explanation

You already know 也 at the **end** of a sentence, marking a statement such as "A is B"
(see [也 ending a nominal sentence](ye-final.md)). 也 can also stand **inside** a
sentence, after its first part. The part before 也 is the **topic**, what the sentence is
about, and the part after it is the **comment** (see [Topic and comment](topic-comment.md)):

- 是語也，盖襲譯歐西人之言也 "as for this expression, it is presumably a translation of what Europeans say".

The topic can be a single noun (是語 "this expression") or a whole clause made into a noun
phrase with 之: 日本人之稱我中國也 "as for the Japanese speaking of our China…"
(see [之 between subject and verb](zhi-nominaliser.md)).

**也者.** A topic can also be closed by 也者, which stresses it more. The pattern
"A 也者，B 也" is common in definitions and contrasts:

- 朝也者，一家之私產也 "a dynasty is the private property of one family".

**Reading tip.** In unpunctuated text, a 也 that is followed by more of the same sentence
usually marks the end of a topic. Put a comma after it. A 也 that ends a sentence takes a
full stop. See [Dividing unpunctuated text (句讀)](judou.md).

## Examples

| Chinese | Pinyin | Translation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 日本人之稱我中國也 | Rìběnrén zhī chēng wǒ Zhōngguó yě | as for the Japanese speaking of our China | u05-p01 |
| 是語也，盖襲譯歐西人之言也 | shì yǔ yě, gài xíyì Ōuxīrén zhī yán yě | as for this expression, it seems to be a translation of what Europeans say | u05-p01 |

| 國也者，人民之公產也 | guó yězhě, rénmín zhī gōngchǎn yě | a state is the public property of the people | u05-p03 |
