## Summary

Words meaning "this" and "that": 此 and 是 "this", 彼 "that", 斯 "this, thus".

## Explanation

**此 cǐ "this"** is the most common. It stands before a noun or on its own:

- before a noun: 此弊 "this fault", 此等 "of this kind, such";
- on its own: 此諭 "this (is my) instruction".

**是 shì "this"** is used in the same way, especially at the start of a sentence
referring back to what has just been said: 是語也 "this expression", 是何言 "what talk is
this?". (In later, more vernacular writing 是 also means "is"; see
[Vernacular elements](vernacular-elements.md).)

**彼 bǐ "that, the other"** often stands opposite 此: 彼此 "that and this", i.e. "both
sides, each other": 彼此並進 "you will both make progress".

**斯 sī "this; thus"** is more formal and less common in this period's prose.

**Demonstratives as pronouns.** All four can stand for a whole idea just mentioned:
吾家宜力矯此弊 "our family must correct this fault" (the fault just described). When a
sentence begins with 此 or 是, look back to the previous sentence to see what it refers to.

## Examples

| Chinese | Pinyin | Translation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 吾家宜力矯此弊 | wú jiā yí lì jiǎo cǐ bì | our family must do its utmost to correct this fault | u04-p02 |
| 此等好學之友，愈多愈好 | cǐděng hàoxué zhī yǒu, yù duō yù hǎo | of friends like this, who love learning, the more the better | u04-p03 |
