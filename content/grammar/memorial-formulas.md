## Summary

The fixed opening, transitions and closing of a memorial to the throne.

## Explanation

**What a memorial is.** A memorial (奏摺 zòuzhé, 摺 for short; also 奏, 疏, 本) is a document
sent by an official to the emperor: a report, a request, an accusation, thanks for an
appointment. From the 18th century the 摺 was the main channel of government. Memorials were
answered by a rescript (硃批, 旨) and often published in the court gazette (京報) or, after
1900, in official gazettes (官報).

**The frame.**

| Part | Formulas | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| summary | 奏爲…事, 爲…恭摺具陳仰祈聖鑒事 | "memorial concerning …": the whole document in one sentence, ending with 事 |
| opening of the body | 竊…, 竊維…, 竊查…, 竊臣… | "humbly, (I) consider / (I) have investigated …" |
| narrative | 欽奉…欽此, 准…咨…前來, 據…稱…等情 | quoting earlier documents: see [Citing earlier documents](embedded-citation.md) |
| summary of the matter | 所有…緣由 | "as to the matter of …" (repeats the summary) |
| submission | 理合恭摺具陳, 理合繕摺具陳 | "it is proper to write out a memorial and report" |
| request | 伏乞皇上聖鑒(訓示) | "prostrate, I beg Your Majesty's inspection (and instructions)" |
| close | 謹奏 | "respectfully memorialised" |
| rescript and date | 某年月日奉硃批/奉旨：…欽此 | the emperor's reply |

**How to read a long memorial.** Read the summary (奏爲…事) first: it tells you what the whole
document is about. Then find the request near the end (擬請, 伏乞). The narrative in between
is often a chain of quoted documents: mark where each begins and ends.

**The writer's voice.** The official calls himself 臣 with his given name (臣之洞), or 奴才
nucai "your slave" if he is a Manchu or bannerman. Joint memorials use 臣等. His acts are
humble (竊, 伏, 謹, 恭); the emperor's are exalted (聖鑒, 聖明, 宸衷) and elevated on the page.
See [Honorific and humble forms](honorific-humble.md).

**Requesting.** 擬請 "I propose to request", 奏請 "to request by memorial", 可否…之處 "whether
… may be permitted", 伏候聖裁 "I humbly await the sacred decision".
