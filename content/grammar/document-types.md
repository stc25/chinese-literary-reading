## Summary

Orders, petitions, communications between equals and rulings, and how to tell them apart.

## Explanation

**Direction decides form.** Every official document travels **up** (to a superior),
**down** (to a subordinate) or **across** (between equals). Its type, its vocabulary and its
closing words follow from that. The Republic of 1912 kept most of the Qing system and renamed
some parts of it.

| Type | Direction | What it is | Typical close |
|---|---|---|---|
| 令 lìng, 命令 | down | an order (from the president, a minister) | 此令 |
| 訓令 xùnlìng | down | an instruction to a subordinate office | 此令 |
| 指令 zhǐlìng | down | an order replying to a subordinate's petition | 此令 |
| 批 pī | down | a ruling written on a petition | 此批 |
| 布告 bùgào, 告示 | down, to the public | a proclamation | 此布, 切切此布 |
| 呈 chéng | up | a petition or report to a superior | 須至呈者, 謹呈 |
| 稟 bǐng | up | (Qing) a petition from a commoner or junior official | |
| 咨 zī | across | a letter between offices of equal rank | 須至咨者 |
| 函 hán, 公函 | across | a letter | |
| 電 diàn | any | a telegram | sender's name and date code |

**How to tell them apart.**

1. **The title.** Gazette headings name the type: 大總統令…文, 實業部批…呈, 內務部呈報…文.
2. **The opening.** A 呈 opens with 為…事 and 竊; a 批 with 據稟, 呈悉 or 稟悉; an order often
   with 據…呈 (quoting the petition it answers); a telegram with its addressees and 鑒.
3. **The words for the reader.** In a document going down, the reader is 該… "the said …"
   (該部, 該代表等). In one going up, the reader is named by title (大總統) and the writer is
   humble (敝部).
4. **The closing words.** See the table.

**Inherited from the Qing.** 呈 and 稟 replace the memorial, and the formulas (竊, 為此呈請,
所有…緣由, 理應…) are the same. The rescript becomes the 批 and the 指令.
