## Summary

未 negates something that has not happened, or not yet: "has not", "not yet".

## Explanation

未 stands before a verb, like 不, but it says that the action **has not (yet) taken place**:

- 憲法未施行 "the constitution has not yet been put into effect".

**未 versus 不.** 不施行 would mean "is not put into effect" (as a fact or a decision);
未施行 means "has not *yet* been put into effect", implying that it may be later.

**未…以前 / 未…之前 "before".** 憲法未施行以前 is literally "before the constitution has
not yet been put into effect". The logic is "in the time when it has not yet…". In English
leave out the negative: "until the constitution has been put into effect".

**未 in pairs.** 未 often stands opposite 既 "already": 未得…，既得… "before (one) has
obtained…, once (one) has obtained…". See [Aspect](aspect-markers.md).
