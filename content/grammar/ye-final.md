## Summary

也 at the end of a sentence marks a statement of the type "A is B".

## Explanation

Literary Chinese has no verb "to be" like the English one. To say "A is B", put the two
side by side and end the sentence with 也:

- 日，太陽也 "日 is the sun".

This is called a **nominal sentence**, because its comment is a noun phrase rather than a verb.

**Words that often come between A and B:**

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 即 | is precisely, that is | 水星即辰星也 "Mercury is Chenxing" |
| 亦 | also | 地球亦行星也 "the earth, too, is a planet" |
| 實 | in fact | 實即中國虛實呆活四義所擴充也 "in fact it is an expansion of China's four categories" |

The negative of a nominal sentence uses 非 "is not": see [非: "is not"](fei-negation.md).

**也 beyond "A is B".** 也 also ends sentences that explain or pass judgement, not only
definitions. What they have in common is a firm, factual statement. In unpunctuated text, a 也
followed by a new sentence is a good place to put a full stop.

**Do not confuse** final 也 with 也 after a topic, inside a sentence: see
[也 after a topic](ye-topic.md).

## Examples

| Chinese | Pinyin | Translation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 水星即辰星也 | Shuǐxīng jí Chénxīng yě | Mercury is Chenxing | u01-p01 |
| 地球亦行星也 | dìqiú yì xíngxīng yě | the earth, too, is a planet | u01-p01 |
| 日：太陽也 | rì: tàiyáng yě | 日: the sun | u01-p02 |
| 月：俗名亮月，即太陰也 | yuè: súmíng liàngyuè, jí tàiyīn yě | 月: popularly called "the bright moon"; that is, the great yin | u01-p02 |
| 實即中國虛實呆活四義所擴充也 | shí jí Zhōngguó xūshí dāihuó sìyì suǒ kuòchōng yě | in fact it is an expansion of China's four categories of "empty" and "full", "dead" and "live" words | u01-p03 |
