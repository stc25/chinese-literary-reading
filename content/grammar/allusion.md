## Summary

References to earlier texts and events, and how to trace them.

## Explanation

**Why it matters.** Writers of this period had memorised the Classics, the histories and a
great deal of poetry, and they expected their readers to have done the same. An allusion
(典故 diǎngù, 用典 yòngdiǎn "using an allusion") lets a writer say a great deal in a few words,
claim the authority of the past, or show learning. If you miss it, you miss part of the meaning.

**Kinds of allusion.**

| Kind | How to spot it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Open quotation | 曰, 云, 有云, 所謂, often with 「」 in modern editions | 子曰：「辭達而已。」 |
| Quotation with a source | a title in 《》 | 《詩》曰：「周雖舊邦，其命維新。」 |
| Embedded phrase | no marker: a phrase that sounds older or more formal than its context | 象寄之才 "interpreters" (from the 禮記) |
| A story or person | a name used as a type | 共工之狂、辛有之痛 |
| A borrowed term | a word from another field used as an image | 前讞 "a precedent" (from the law courts) |

**Common sources.** The Four Books (論語, 孟子, 大學, 中庸), the Five Classics (詩, 書, 易,
禮記, 春秋 with the 左傳), the 史記 and the other histories, the philosophers (莊子, 荀子),
and Tang and Song poetry.

**How to trace an allusion.**

1. **Notice** a phrase that seems out of place, too compressed, or marked with 曰 or 所謂.
2. **Search** the exact phrase in a full-text database: the Chinese Text Project
   (ctext.org) for pre-modern texts, Wikisource (zh.wikisource.org), or the 漢語大詞典 for
   phrases with a long history. Search a short distinctive sequence of four or five characters, not the whole line.
3. **Compare** the wording. Writers quoted from memory and adapted freely: Yan Fu writes
   修辭立誠 for 修辭立其誠; Wang Guowei writes 西風 for 秋風. Note whether the change matters.
4. **Read the context** of the original. Is the writer using it in the same sense, or
   turning it (斷章取義 "cutting a passage to take a meaning", as Wang Guowei does with love poems)?
5. **Ask what it adds**: authority, irony, a contrast between past and present?

**Modern editions.** Commentaries and annotated editions (注本) usually identify allusions;
use them, but check their references, which are sometimes wrong.

## Examples

| Chinese | Pinyin | Translation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 子曰：「辭達而已。」 | zǐ yuē: cí dá ér yǐ. | the Master said: words should get the meaning across, that is all (論語, 衛靈公) | u12-p01 |
| 《詩》曰：「周雖舊邦，其命維新。」 | Shī yuē: Zhōu suī jiù bāng, qí mìng wéi xīn. | the *Odes* say: though Zhou is an old state, its mandate is new | u09-p02 |
| 豈所謂「老大嫁作商人婦」者耶 | qǐ suǒwèi lǎodà jià zuò shāngrén fù zhě yé | is this not what is called "grown old and married to a merchant" (白居易, 琵琶行)? | u10-p03 |
| 「衣帶漸寬終不悔，爲伊消得人憔悴。」此第二境也 | yīdài jiàn kuān zhōng bù huǐ, wèi yī xiāodé rén qiáocuì. cǐ dì-èr jìng yě | "my sash grows looser, but I never regret it; for her I waste away": this is the second stage (柳永, 鳳棲梧) | u12-p03 |
| 而可據為前讞者，特《黑奴籲天錄》耳 | ér kě jù wéi qiányàn zhě, tè Hēinú Yùtiān Lù ěr | the only precedent I can rely on is *Uncle Tom's Cabin* | u12-p02 |
