## Summary

How a document quotes the documents it responds to, and where the quotation ends.

## Explanation

**Why it matters.** Official documents are answers. An order quotes the petition it answers;
a petition quotes the order it is carrying out; a report quotes the letters that led to it. A
long document can be mostly quotation, sometimes quotation within quotation. The original has
no quotation marks, so you must find the opening and closing formulas.

**Opening formulas.** The verb tells you who wrote the quoted document:

| Formula | Quoting | From |
|---|---|---|
| 欽奉上諭, 奉旨, 欽奉懿旨 | an edict, a rescript | the emperor, the Empress Dowager |
| 奉…令 | an order | a superior |
| 准…咨(開), 准…函 | a letter | an equal |
| 據…呈(稱), 據…稟(稱), 據稟 | a petition | a subordinate or a member of the public |
| 據…電(稱) | a telegram | anyone |
| 茲據…奏稱 | a memorial | an official (in an edict) |

**Closing formulas.** The closing word usually matches the direction:

| Formula | Closes |
|---|---|
| 欽此 | an imperial document |
| 等因 | a document from a superior or an equal |
| 等由 | a letter from an equal |
| 等情 | a petition from a subordinate |
| 等語 | a summary of what was said |
| 前來 | added after 等因/等情/等由: "which has reached me" |
| 在案 | "as on record" (closing a reference to an earlier act) |

Then the writer often marks receipt: 准此 "having received this", 奉此 "having received this
(from a superior)", 據此 "on this basis". What follows is his own response.

**Nested citations.** A petition may quote an order that quoted a petition. Work from the
outside in: find the outermost writer, then each 據/准/奉 and its matching 等因/等情.

**Summary or quotation?** 據…稱 often introduces a summary rather than an exact quotation.
Modern editions put both in quotation marks; treat them with care.
