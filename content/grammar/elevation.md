## Summary

Starting a new line or leaving a space before words for the emperor, the state or a superior: a mark of respect, and a clue to the structure of a document.

## Explanation

**What it is.** In official documents of the Qing and early Republic, words referring to the
emperor, his acts, the court and its institutions were set apart on the page. This
practice is called 抬頭 táitóu "raising the head". In vertical text there were several degrees:

| Form | How it looks | Typical words |
|---|---|---|
| 空抬 kōngtái "blank elevation" | a blank space before the word, in the same column | 恩准, 部, 欽命 |
| 平抬 píngtái "level elevation" | the word starts a new column, level with the others | 上諭, 朝廷 |
| 單抬 dāntái "single elevation" | a new column, raised one character above the others | 皇上, 聖 |
| 雙抬 shuāngtái / 三抬 sāntái | raised two or three characters | 皇帝, 皇太后, 天, 祖宗 |

The more exalted the referent, the higher the word was raised.

**In the Treaty of Nanjing.** The transcription shows blank spaces before 恩准 "graciously
grants" (an act of the emperor) and 部 "the Board" (an imperial ministry). The gap before 大英國
in article 2 suggests that the British sovereign's country was given elevation too: in treaty
texts, this kind of equal treatment on the page mattered.

**Not every gap is elevation.** A gap can also be a **blank** left for a figure or a name to be
filled in, as in 每兩加稅不過　分 "not more than __ fen per tael". Ask whether the word after
the gap deserves respect.

**Why it matters for reading.**

- An elevated word usually begins a new phrase: a likely break in unpunctuated text.
- Elevation tells you who is being honoured, and so who is writing to whom.
- Modern punctuated editions usually remove it. If your argument depends on it, check a facsimile.

**Related practices.** 避諱 bìhuì "taboo avoidance": characters in the reigning emperor's
personal name were avoided, replaced by another character or written with a stroke missing
(e.g. 玄 written 元 in the Kangxi era). Humble words for oneself (臣, 竊) were sometimes written
smaller or to the right of the column.
