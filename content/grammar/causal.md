## Summary

Words that mark a reason (因 "because") or a result (故, 是以 "therefore").

## Explanation

**Reason: 因 "because, because of".** 因 stands at the start of the clause giving the reason;
the result follows:

- 因大英商船遠路涉洋…，自應給予沿海一處 "because British merchant ships travel long distances…,
  they should naturally be given a place on the coast";
- 伯兄撫屏，因中風不語 "my eldest brother Fuping, because of a stroke, could not speak".

Other ways to give a reason: 以 + clause "because" (以其能群耳 "because they can form societies");
為 wèi "because of"; 由 "from, owing to".

**Result: 故, 是以, 是故 "therefore".** These stand at the start of the clause that draws the conclusion:

- 故夫變者，古今之公理也 "therefore change is the universal principle of past and present";
- 是以君子欲為人神所憑依，莫大於習勞也 "therefore, if the noble person wishes to be relied on…".

是以 is literally "by this" and 是故 "for this reason"; 故 alone is the most common.

**故 as a noun.** 故 also means "reason, cause" (無故 "without reason") and "old, former" (故鄉
"home town"). At the start of a clause, after a full stop or comma, it is usually "therefore".

**Not 所以.** In this period 所以 usually means "that by which, the reason why", not "therefore". See
[所以](suoyi.md).

## Examples

| Chinese | Pinyin | Translation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 故夫變者，古今之公理也 | gù fú biàn zhě, gǔjīn zhī gōnglǐ yě | therefore change is the universal principle of past and present | u09-p01 |
