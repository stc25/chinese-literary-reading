## Summary

Words meaning "if": 若, 如, 苟, 使, 倘 (and 藉曰 "supposing"); the result is often introduced by 則 or 斯.

## Explanation

**Often there is no "if".** Most conditions are not marked at all: the first clause states
the condition and 則 introduces the result (see [則](ze.md)):

- 夜不炳燭則昧 "(if) at night you do not light a candle, it is dark".

**Marked conditions.** When a writer wants to stress the condition, one of these words opens it:

| Word | Notes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 若 ruò | the most common "if" | 若實看明白了… "if you really understand it…" |
| 如 rú | "if"; also "like" | 如其老大也 "if it is old…" |
| 苟 gǒu | "if (only), provided that" | 苟其子孫達於此義 "if their descendants understand this principle" |
| 使 shǐ | "if, supposing"; also "to make" | — |
| 倘 tǎng | "if, in case", especially in documents | 倘有按期未能交足之數 "if any amount is not paid in full on time" |
| 藉曰 jièyuē | "supposing (that)" | 藉曰不變 "supposing they did not change" |

**The result.** After a marked condition, the result is introduced by 則 "then", 斯 "then,
thus", or 即 "then, at once": 苟其子孫達於此義…，斯號中興矣 "if their descendants
understand this principle…, then this is called a restoration".

**使 is two words.** 使 + person + verb is usually "make someone do something" (使之處勞 "make
them live in hardship"). As "if", 使 opens a clause and is followed by a whole condition.

**如 is two words, too.** 如 + noun usually means "like": 老年人如夕照 "the old are like the evening sun".
