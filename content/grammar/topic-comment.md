## Summary

Sentences often name a topic first and then say something about it.

## Explanation

Many sentences in literary Chinese have two parts:

1. the **topic**: what the sentence is about, at the start;
2. the **comment**: what is said about the topic.

Often the topic is also the subject in the English sense: in 月借日光為光 "the moon borrows the
sun's light", 月 is both. But the topic need not be the subject of the verb that follows:

- 對日一面有光: topic 對日一面 "the side facing the sun", comment 有光 "there is light".
  In English: "the side facing the sun is lit".

The comment can be a verb phrase (有光 "there is light") or a noun phrase, as in nominal sentences such as 地球亦行星也 "the earth (topic) is also a planet (comment)"
(see [也 ending a nominal sentence](ye-final.md)).

**Reading tip.** When a sentence seems to have no subject, or a subject that cannot
do the action, look for a topic at the start. Translate it with "as for …" first, then
rephrase in natural English.

**Marking the topic.** A topic is often left unmarked, as in the examples here. In
later units you will see topics closed by 也 or 者: see [也 after a topic](ye-topic.md).

## Examples

| Chinese | Pinyin | Translation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 對日一面有光 | duì rì yīmiàn yǒu guāng | the side facing the sun is lit (lit. as for the side facing the sun, there is light) | u01-p02 |
| 背日一面無光 | bèi rì yīmiàn wú guāng | the side turned away from the sun is dark | u01-p02 |
| 地球亦行星也 | dìqiú yì xíngxīng yě | the earth, too, is a planet | u01-p01 |
