## Summary

雖 means "although, even if" and 縱 "even if"; the second clause often contains 亦 "still" or 而 "yet".

## Explanation

**雖 "although, even if".** 雖 opens the clause that concedes something. It stands **after
the subject** if there is one:

- 周雖舊邦，其命維新 "though Zhou is an old state, its mandate is new";
- 雖極忙，亦須了本日功課 "even when you are extremely busy, you must still finish each day's lessons".

Whether 雖 means "although" (a fact) or "even if" (a possibility) depends on the context.

**The second clause.** The main clause often contains a word that answers 雖:

| Word | Example |
|---|---|
| 亦 "still, also" | 雖走路之日，到店亦可看 "even on a travelling day you can read once you reach the inn" |
| 然 "but" | 四妹生產雖難，然血暈亦是常事 "although Fourth Sister's delivery was difficult, fainting is a common thing" (Zeng Guofan, letter to his parents) |
| 而 "and yet" | 雖…，而… |

**雖然 "even so, nevertheless".** At the start of a sentence, 雖然 is "although this is so",
i.e. "nevertheless". (In later vernacular, 雖然 means "although".)

**縱 "even if".** 縱 is stronger and nearly always hypothetical: "even if (it were the case that)".
It is less common than 雖.
