## Summary

矣 at the end of a sentence marks a change or a new situation: something is now, or will then be, different.

## Explanation

**矣 versus 也.** Both end sentences, but they do different jobs:

| Particle | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 也 | a fact or definition: "A is B" | 地球亦行星也 "the earth, too, is a planet" |
| 矣 | a change: "has become, is now" | 伯兄病已革矣 "my brother's illness had become critical" |

**What 矣 can express.**

- **A completed change**, often with 已: 已購船票矣 "I had already bought the tickets".
- **A result that will follow**, often with 則 and 必: 則數年必成書家矣 "then in a few years you
  will surely have become a calligrapher"; 則彼此並進矣 "then you will both make progress".
- **A conclusion**: 明矣 "(it is) clear (now)": 則不能以朝之老少而指爲國之老少明矣 "it is
  clear that one cannot take the age of a dynasty as the age of the state".

**In questions.** 矣乎 combines 矣 with the question particle 乎: 我中國其果老大矣乎 "has our China
really grown old?", asking about a change.

**Reading tip.** In unpunctuated text, 矣 almost always ends a sentence or clause: put a full
stop (or semicolon) after it.
