## Summary

How treaty articles are framed, and how the Chinese text relates to the other language.

## Explanation

**The parts of a treaty.**

| Part | Typical wording |
|---|---|
| preamble: the parties and their plenipotentiaries | 大清皇帝、大英君主…特簡…為全權大臣 |
| examination of full powers | 各將所奉全權…互相較閱，俱屬妥當 |
| articles | 第一款, 第二款 … (款 "article" in treaties, 條 in laws) |
| ratification and exchange | 本約…御筆批准…互換 |
| signature and seal | 為此兩國全權大臣署名蓋印，以昭信守 |
| date, often in both calendars | 光緒二十一年三月二十三日 / 明治二十八年四月十七日 |
| separate or additional articles | 另約, 專條, 議定專條 |

**Treaty vocabulary.**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 約, 條約, 和約, 章程 | treaty, peace treaty, regulations |
| 全權大臣 | plenipotentiary |
| 彼此約明, 議定 | it is mutually agreed |
| 仍留照行 | remains in force |
| 作為廢紙, 廢絶 | null and void, abolished |
| 批准互換 | exchange of ratifications |
| 正文 | authentic text |
| 為憑, 作為正義 | to be the authority, the correct meaning |
| 以昭信守 | in witness whereof ("to show good faith") |
| 一體均霑 | (most-favoured-nation) "to share equally" |

**Two languages, two texts.** Treaties with Western powers and Japan were drawn up in two or
three languages, and they did not always say the same thing. A treaty may name an
**authoritative text**:

- the Treaty of Tianjin (1858): English prevails (總以英文作為正義);
- the Treaty of Shimonoseki (1895): an English text referees between Chinese and Japanese.

**Reading a Chinese treaty text critically.**

1. **Find the other version.** English texts are in Hertslet's *China Treaties* and the
   Customs collection *Treaties, Conventions, etc.* (1917); Chinese texts in collections such as 《中外舊約章彙編》.
2. **Compare clause by clause.** Look for things in one text and not the other, for vague
   Chinese where the English is precise (酌宜 against "at the option of Japan"), and for loaded words (夷).
3. **Ask which text the parties relied on.** In a dispute, the authoritative text decides,
   but Chinese officials and the public read the Chinese.

**Translation style.** Chinese treaty texts often turn a Western legal phrase into a
four-character pair (完全無缺之獨立自主 "full and complete independence and autonomy") or
into a clause (凡有虧損獨立自主體制 "in derogation of such independence").
