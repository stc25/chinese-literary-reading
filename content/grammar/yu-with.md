## Summary

與 means "with" before a verb, or "and" between nouns.

## Explanation

**"With".** 與 + noun before a verb says who else takes part in the action:

- 與各該地方官公文往來 "exchange official documents **with** the local officials";
- 爾須與之互相勸勉 "you must encourage each other (**with** them)".

As with other prepositions, the 與-phrase stands **before** the verb.

**"And".** Between two nouns, 與 joins them: 日與月 "the sun and the moon", 老年與少年 "the
old and the young". Other words for "and" are 及 and 暨, which join nouns, especially in
lists and documents.

**"And" or "with"?** If what follows the second noun is a verb that both nouns do together,
"and" and "with" mean much the same: 朝與國既異物 "since a dynasty and a state are different things".

**Comparisons.** 與 A 等 "equal to A", 與 A 相似 "similar to A": 本約法之效力與憲法等
"this Provisional Constitution has the same force as a constitution".
