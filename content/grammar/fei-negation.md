## Summary

非 negates a statement of identity: "A is not B".

## Explanation

To say "A is B", literary Chinese puts A and B side by side, usually with 也 (see
[也 ending a nominal sentence](ye-final.md)). To say "A is **not** B", put 非 between them:

> **A 非 B (也)。** "A is not B."

- 今之世非昔之世 "the present age is not the age of the past".

A final 也 is common but not required.

**非 versus 不.** 不 negates verbs and adjectives: 不動 "does not move", 不外 "does not go
beyond" (see [不: negating verbs](bu-negation.md)). 非 negates an identity between nouns.
If you can translate the clause with "is not" and a noun phrase, expect 非.

**非 in other patterns.** You will later meet:

- 非…不… "not … unless …", "only if …";
- 非…也 at the start of an explanation: "it is not that…".

## Examples

| Chinese | Pinyin | Translation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 今之世非昔之世 | jīn zhī shì fēi xī zhī shì | the world of today is not the world of the past | u02-p03 |
| 今之人非昔之人 | jīn zhī rén fēi xī zhī rén | the people of today are not the people of the past | u02-p03 |
