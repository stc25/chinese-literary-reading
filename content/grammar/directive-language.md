## Summary

How an official document tells its recipient what to do.

## Explanation

**Ordering.** A document going down rarely says "you must". It uses a small set of directive
words, each with its own force:

| Word | Pinyin | Force | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 著 | zhuó | (edicts) "let …": an imperial command | 著即自丙午科為始 |
| 仰 | yǎng | "you are directed to" (to a subordinate) | 仰該代表等迅卽遵照 |
| 飭 | chì | "to order" (a subordinate office) | 飭交揚州民政長確查 |
| 令 | lìng | "to order" | 令行該部 |
| 合就 / 合行 | héjiù / héxíng | "it is proper that I hereby …" (introducing the decision) | 合就令行該部 |
| 應 | yīng | "should, is to" | 應計畆、繪圖 |
| 須 | xū | "must" | 須加部署 |
| 不得 / 毋 | bùdé / wú | "may not", "do not" | 毌任毀壞散失 |
| 准 | zhǔn | "is permitted" | 應准暫由財政部從權辦理 |

**Complying.** The recipient is told to 遵照 "comply", 查照 "note and act accordingly",
遵辦 "carry out", 知照 "take note". Speed is added with 即, 迅即, 速, 克日 "within the set days".

**Softer forms.** Between equals or to the public, orders become requests: 希 "(I) hope",
請 "please", 尚希 "I further hope", 務希 "I earnestly hope". Upwards, a writer can only
request: 呈請, 擬請, 伏乞.

**Purpose.** Orders end with their purpose: 以重文敎而保公產 "so as to respect education and
protect public property", 以便…, 以期…, 俾…. Look for 以 near the end of an order.

| 合就令行該部，速派妥員 | héjiù lìngxíng gāi bù, sù pài tuǒ yuán | it is proper that I hereby order the said ministries to send reliable officials quickly | u14-p01 |
