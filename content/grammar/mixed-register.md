## Summary

Writing that moves between literary and vernacular styles, and why.

## Explanation

**A spectrum, not two languages.** Between about 1895 and 1930, Chinese prose ran along a
spectrum. It helps to distinguish four points on it:

| Style | Grammar | Vocabulary | Example in this course |
|---|---|---|---|
| classical (古文) | literary | classical, allusive | Lin Shu's letter to Cai Yuanpei ([U20](../units/u20-literary-and-vernacular/u20-p02-lin-shu-defends-the-classics.md)); 秋水軒尺牘 ([U19](../units/u19-letters-and-diaries/u19-p03-letters-from-autumn-water-studio.md)) |
| new literary (新文體, 新民體) | literary | many new terms; parallel and emphatic | Liang Qichao ([U16](../units/u16-the-press-and-new-vocabulary/u16-p01-a-new-history.md)); Chen Duxiu ([U20](../units/u20-literary-and-vernacular/u20-p01-on-literary-revolution.md)) |
| plain literary (淺近文言) | literary, simple | modern and some vernacular words | Republican documents and newspapers ([U14](../units/u14-republican-documents/unit.md)); Cai Yuanpei ([U20](../units/u20-literary-and-vernacular/u20-p03-cai-yuanpei-replies.md)) |
| vernacular (白話) | vernacular | modern | the new literature after 1918 (not in this course) |

Most texts a researcher meets from the period are in the middle two styles.

**How to read a mixed text.**

1. **Take the grammar from the function words.** If the text uses 之, 者, 也, 矣, 乎, 其, 所, 以,
   read it as literary Chinese, even if its nouns are modern.
2. **Take the date and stance from the vocabulary.** New terms (主義, 社會, 形式, 內容) date a
   text; vernacular particles (的, 是否, 了) show a writer willing to come close to speech.
3. **Ask why the writer chose this register.** A petition, a treaty or a letter to an elder
   called for literary Chinese whatever one's views. A manifesto for a new literature written
   in literary Chinese, as Chen Duxiu's was, reached readers who expected that register.

**Register as argument.** In the language debate of 1917–1920, the choice of register was itself
a position. Lin Shu defended the classical language in pure classical prose. Cai Yuanpei answered
in literary Chinese with vernacular words (是否, 白話的文字), and argued that the two registers
differ only in form. Chen Duxiu demanded a literary revolution in the new literary style, with 的
in his slogans. The vernacular reformers themselves went on using literary Chinese for formal
letters, prefaces and scholarship for decades.

**Mixed registers within a text.** Registers can also change inside one text:

- **quotation**: a literary text may quote speech in the vernacular (court records, novels);
- **level**: a letter may use literary formulas in its frame and plainer language in its body
  (Zeng Guofan's family letters, [U19](../units/u19-letters-and-diaries/u19-p02-to-my-parents-to-my-sons.md));
- **emphasis**: a vernacular word can stand out against literary prose, as 的 does in Chen's slogans.
