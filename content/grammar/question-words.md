## Summary

Words meaning "what", "how", "why", "where", "who" and "which": 何, 安, 烏, 焉, 奚, 孰, 誰.

## Explanation

**The question word stands where the answer would go.** If the answer would be the object,
the question word is often placed **before** the verb:

- 國也者，何物也？ "a state is *what* thing?" (the answer would replace 何物);
- 是何言？ "this is *what* talk?", i.e. "what talk is this?".

As an object of a verb or preposition, 何 usually comes before it: 何以 "by what, why",
何為 "for what, why".

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 何 hé | what; why; how | 何物 "what thing", 何以 "why", 何言 "what talk" |
| 安 ān, 烏 wū, 焉 yān | how? where? (often rhetorical) | 烏知其可 "how can I know it will do?" |
| 奚 xī | what? why? | — |
| 孰 shú | which (of several)? who? | — |
| 誰 shuí | who? | — |

**何 in fixed expressions.**

| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 何以 | why, how (lit. "by what") | 
| 何如 / 何若 | what is it like? how about…? |
| 若何 | how? |
| 何有 | what is there? (rhetorical: "nothing") |
| 何一非 | which one is not…? (rhetorical: "all are") |

**Question words are often rhetorical.** 烏知其可 does not ask for information; it means "I
doubt that it will do". See [Rhetorical questions](rhetorical-questions.md).
