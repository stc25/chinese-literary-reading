## Summary

Verbs of possibility, ability, permission and necessity, placed directly before the main verb.

## Explanation

Like English "can", "must" and "should", these words stand **before** the verb they modify:

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 能 néng | can, be able to | 余不能與弟相見 "I cannot see you" |
| 可 kě | may, can; (it) is acceptable | 到店亦可看 "you can read once you reach the inn" |
| 得 dé | may, be allowed to | 人民得享有…自由權 "the people may enjoy … civil liberties" |
| 足 zú | be enough to, be worth | 足慰遠懷 "is enough to comfort (me) far away" |
| 宜 yí | should, it is fitting to | 宜作還里之計 "you should plan to return home" |
| 當 dāng | ought to | 吾人所當保存 "what we ought to preserve" |
| 須 xū | must, need to | 但須有恆 "but you must persevere" |
| 必 bì | certainly, necessarily | 數年必成書家 "in a few years you will certainly become a calligrapher" |

**Negatives.** 不能 "cannot"; 不可 "must not, cannot"; 不得 "may not" (in laws: 不得逮捕
"may not arrest"); 不必 "need not"; 不宜 "should not" (不宜留連鄂中 "should not linger in Hubei").

**可 on its own.** 可 can end a clause: 習字臨《千字文》亦可 "copying the Thousand Character
Classic is also fine". 可 also forms adjectives: 可愛 "lovable", 可畏 "fearsome".

**必 is not "must".** 必 usually means "certainly, will surely", describing what is bound to
happen: 有衝突則必有調和 "where there is conflict there will necessarily be reconciliation". For an
obligation, look for 須, 宜 or 當.
