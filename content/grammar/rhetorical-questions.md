## Summary

Questions that expect no answer, such as 豈…哉 and 而況…乎; they are a forceful way of making a statement.

## Explanation

Essays of this period are full of questions that do not ask anything. To understand one,
work out the answer the writer expects, and read the question as that statement.

| Pattern | Example | Expected answer, i.e. the meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 豈 … 哉/耶/乎？ "how could…? is it not…?" | 豈所謂「老大嫁作商人婦」者耶？ | yes, it is exactly that |
| 豈有他哉？ "how could it be anything else?" | 今吾中國所以日即衰落者，豈有他哉？ | it is nothing else |
| A 且然，而況 B 乎/耶？ "if even A…, how much more B?" | 美人豪傑且然，而況於尋常碌碌者耶？ | B certainly is |
| 何…之有 / 何有？ "what is there?" | 則個人之自由更何有也？ | nothing |
| 何一非…哉？ "which one is not…?" | 何一非…之陳跡哉！ | every one is |
| V 乎不 V？ | 能乎不能？ | no, he cannot |
| 不亦…乎？ "is it not also…?" | — | yes, it is |
| 烏/安/焉 + verb "how could…?" | 烏知其可？ | I could not |

**豈 is the key word.** When 豈 appears near the start of a clause, expect a rhetorical question,
usually expecting "no" to a negative idea or "yes" to a positive one. Translate it "how
could…?", "surely…", or "is it not…?".

**Punctuation.** Modern editions may end a rhetorical question with ？ or ！: 何一非…哉！.
The editor's choice is an interpretation; the particle (哉, 耶, 乎) is what the author wrote.

| 美人豪傑且然，而況於尋常碌碌者耶 | měirén háojié qiě rán, érkuàng yú xúncháng lùlù zhě yē | if even beautiful women and heroes are like this, how much more so are ordinary, mediocre people! | u10-p03 |
