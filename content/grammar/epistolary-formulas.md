## Summary

The fixed openings, transitions and closings of letters, and how they change with the direction of the letter.

## Explanation

**The frame.** A traditional letter (書, 札, 函, 尺牘) has a frame of fixed parts around its
content. Each part has its own set of formulas, chosen by the relationship between writer and
recipient.

| Part | Name | Upwards (to a superior) | Between equals | Downwards (to a junior) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| address | 稱謂 | 中堂, 大人, 父母親大人 | 某某仁兄, 某兄 | 某某兒, 某弟 |
| address term | 提稱語 | 鈞座, 鈞鑒, 尊前, 膝下 (to parents) | 台鑒, 大鑒, 足下, 如晤 | 知悉, 覽 |
| opening | 啟事語 | 敬稟者, 謹稟者 | 敬啟者, 逕啟者 | 字諭, 字示 |
| closing | 結束語 | 肅此具稟, 肅此 | 專此, 草此 | 此囑, 此諭 |
| good wishes | 祝頌語 | 恭請鈞安, 恭叩鈞綏, 敬請福安 | 順頌時祺, 即請台安 | (none, or 即問近好) |
| signature | 署名 | given name + 謹稟, 男某跪稟 | 弟某頓首, 弟某拜 | 父示, 某手諭, style name + 示 |

**Direction words.** The verb of writing tells you the direction at once:

| Upwards | Between equals | Downwards |
|---|---|---|
| 稟 "report", 上 "submit", 呈 "present" | 啟 "open, inform", 致 "send", 書 "write" | 諭 "instruct", 示 "show", 字 "write to" |

**Opening greetings.** After the opening comes a greeting about the time apart or the season
(別後駒光如駛 "since we parted, time has flown"; 久不見…… "I have long not seen …"), then thanks
for the last letter (接到家書, 近得……札), then the business.

**Closing phrases.** Before the good wishes, a phrase apologises for the letter's brevity or
expresses the writer's feeling: 不盡所懷, 不盡欲言 "I cannot say all I wish", 因風寄意 "I send my
thoughts by the wind", 諸宜珍攝 "take good care of yourself".

**Dates.** A letter usually gives only month and day after the signature (七月初四日). Readers must
work out the year from the contents or the collection.

**The honorific gap.** In manuscripts and older printings, a word referring to the recipient (鈞,
台, 尊) may be preceded by a blank space (挪抬) or moved to the top of a new column (平抬). Modern
editions close the gaps.

**Manuals.** Letter-writing manuals (尺牘大全, 尺牘範本) gave the formulas for every relationship and
occasion, with model letters. Even Republican writers who wrote in the vernacular kept many of these
formulas in their letters.
