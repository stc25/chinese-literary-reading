## Summary

所以 means "that by which" or "the reason why"; it makes a noun phrase, unlike modern "therefore".

## Explanation

所以 is 所 "that which" + 以 "by means of, because of": literally "that by which". So
所以 + verb is a **noun phrase** with two related meanings:

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| the means by which | 衡所以權權利之輕重 "the scales are (that) by which (one) weighs rights" |
| the reason why | 人之所以貴於他物者 "the reason why human beings are nobler than other creatures" |

**The frame "A 之所以 V 者，B 也".** "The reason why A Vs is B":

- 人之所以貴於他物者，以其能群耳 "the reason human beings are nobler than other
  creatures is simply that they can form societies".

之 turns A + 所以 V into a noun phrase, 者 closes it, and the comment gives the reason,
often with 以 "because" or 由 "from".

**所以 as "the means" at the end.** 道德之立，所以利群也 "morality is established as
the means of benefiting society": "…is that by which (we) benefit society".

**Not "therefore".** In later and vernacular Chinese, 所以 at the start of a clause means
"therefore". In literary prose of this period, first try "that by which" or "the reason
why"; "therefore" is usually expressed by 故 or 是以.
