## Summary

有 means "there is, to have" and 無 "there is no, to lack"; both are verbs and take an object.

## Explanation

有 and 無 are a pair of opposite verbs. The thing that exists, or is had, follows as the object:

| | "to have" | "there is" |
|---|---|---|
| 有 | 人民有請願於議會之權 "the people have the right to petition the assembly" | 有衝突則必有調和 "where there is conflict there will be reconciliation" |
| 無 | 行星無光 "planets have no light" | 無種族、階級、宗教之區別 "(there is) no distinction of race, class or religion" |

English chooses between "have" and "there is"; Chinese does not need to. If there is a
topic before 有/無, "have" usually works best; if not, use "there is".

**無 is not 不.** 無 is a verb meaning "not have"; 不 is an adverb meaning "not". So 無光 is "has
no light", but 不光 would be "is not bright". See [不: negating verbs](bu-negation.md).

**有…在** "there is … (right here)": 吾心目中有一少年中國在 "in my mind's eye there is a young China".

**In fixed expressions.** 無上 "with nothing above", 至高無上 "highest of all".
