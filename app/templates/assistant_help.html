Using the assistant critically

The reading assistant helps you read a text that is not in the course. It works through the same steps as the course passages, and it tells you, for every suggestion, where the suggestion comes from. Use it to save time on what you can check easily, and spend your own attention on what it cannot do reliably.

The method

  1. Text. Read the text through. If it has no punctuation, decide where the clauses end ({{ course.grammar['judou'].title }}). The assistant can suggest punctuation, but the breaks decide the meaning, so check each one.
  2. Words. Look up every word you do not know. Words taught in the course are glossed from the course glossary; the AI glosses the rest. Where it explains a whole phrase rather than a word, the item is marked phrase: work out its words yourself.
  3. Grammar. Identify the function words (之, 者, 所, 以, 而, 則 …) and what each does in its clause. The grammar pages explain them.
  4. Names, dates and allusions. Identify people, places, offices, books and dates, and any phrase that refers to an earlier text.
  5. Translation. Write your own translation, then compare it with the AI's.

What the AI does well

  • Common words and phrases, and the general sense of a sentence.
  • Suggesting punctuation for straightforward prose.
  • Explaining a construction when you ask a precise question about it.

How it goes wrong

These examples are from tests of the assistant on texts of the period.

  • It cuts words in the wrong place. It glossed 希 on its own ("hopeful") in 希望 "hope". Check that each glossed word is really a word in this text.
  • It invents identifications. Given 吾心目中有一少年中國在, it called 少年中國 "a young China" the title of an essay by Chen Duxiu (1915). The sentence comes from Liang Qichao's 〈少年中國說〉 (1900). Names, titles and allusions are where it is most often wrong.
  • It breaks clauses in the wrong place. It punctuated 有一少年中國。在欲言國之老少, cutting off 在, which ends the first clause: 吾心目中有一少年中國在。
  • It gets numbers and dates wrong. Asked about 光緒二十六年, it gave 1899; the year is 1900. This is why dates are converted by the course's date converter, never by the AI.
  • It chooses the wrong grammar point. In 天演者，物競天擇之謂也 it called 者 a nominaliser, where the course reads 者…也 as a definition. Compare its choice with the grammar page.
  • It uses modern senses. Words such as 經濟, 社會 or 革命 had other meanings before 1900 (New terms).
  • It sounds sure when it is guessing. A fluent answer is not a checked one.

How to check

  • Words: the course glossary and the dictionaries in Script and dictionaries.
  • Grammar: the grammar pages.
  • Dates: Dates and calendars.
  • People and offices: Names, titles and offices and the biographical databases in Research resources.
  • Quotations and allusions: search the phrase in the full-text databases in Research resources.

Limits and privacy

  • Texts of up to 600 characters; analyse longer texts in parts.
  • Up to {{ daily }} AI suggestions a day (one full analysis uses five, each question one), and 10 questions per text. The course steps have no limit. A suggestion that fails because the AI service is unavailable does not count.
  • If many people use the assistant at once, it may ask you to try again in a minute.
  • The AI steps send your text to an external AI service, which does not keep it. This site stores nothing you paste. Do not paste personal or confidential material.
